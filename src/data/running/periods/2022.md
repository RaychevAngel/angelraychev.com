In spring 2022 I was eighteen, finishing school and training for the middle distances.
I graduated from twelfth grade in May. The year produced the 800 m and 1500 m
marks I still use as historical reference points: **2:03.62** and **4:23.90**.

Those numbers came after a progression. A January 1000 m time trial was 3:05;
a February 1500 was 4:47. In April I ran an 800 time trial in 2:12. In May I
went under 4:30 for 1500. June brought the men's national championships, and
July brought another two-day championship weekend in the U20 category.

The notes preserve more than improving times. They contain repeated workouts,
my first session in spikes, pacing failures and several returns after injury.
Together with the old training sheet, they let me look at how the season
actually developed, rather than remember only its fastest days.

In the May 1000 I finished third in series 1. The final combined result was
11th. My [activity note](https://www.strava.com/activities/7180607857) called it

I was very happy with **2:03.62**, despite the wet track.

The dates have needed a correction. The 1500 activity is entered under 12 June,
but the federation table puts the event on Saturday, **11 June**. That agrees
with my recollection that the 1500 was on day one and the 800 on day two.
The race date here follows the competition record.

July adds a useful second reference. On
[6 July](https://www.strava.com/activities/7428615611), after another injury,
I ran 600 m in **1:32.77** — **2:34.6/km** — and hoped it would be enough for personal bests
that weekend. The races were **4:25.96** and **2:04.54**, just over two seconds
and just under one second slower than the June marks. I finished sixth in
[1500 series 2](https://www.bfla.org/results/2022/2022/19_u20/men/1500.htm)
and sixth in
[800 series 3](https://www.bfla.org/results/2022/2022/19_u20/men/800.htm),
with seventh in each championship classification. The June performances had
another pair of official results close behind them.

## Selected workouts

The complete list of recorded runs is below. These sessions explain several
parts of the season that a list of best times would miss. Distances and rests
come from the workout notes; unrecorded recovery remains unrecorded.

### Winter work and the mixed-rest template

<span id="19-january--the-mixed-rest-fast-template"></span>On [19 January](https://www.strava.com/activities/6548852405), I ran
**4×200 m with three-minute rests, then 4×200 m with 90-second rests**.

First set: **30.3 · 30.5 · 29.6 · 29.9**.
Second set: **32.8 · 33.0 · 33.1 · 32.8**.

This is the old session behind the mixed-rest fast template in the
[current notebook](/running-notebook/). The second set was slower after the
earlier fast work and with the shorter recovery. Keeping both sets makes the reference more useful than remembering
only a sub-30-second repetition.

The winter and early spring also contained substantial longer work. On
[21 February](https://www.strava.com/activities/6718281972), **9×1000 m / 4 min**
went **4:02 · 3:59 · 3:58 · 3:56 · 3:56 · 3:57 · 3:55 · 3:50 · 3:47**.
The 1000 m splits are also their paces per kilometre; the set averaged about **3:56/km**. I wrote that I still felt fresh after the final repetition. On
[14 March](https://www.strava.com/activities/6822877799), **4×3000 m / 6 min**
went **11:59 · 11:50 · 11:42 · 11:37**, or about **4:00/km, 3:57/km, 3:54/km, 3:52/km**. The fast work belonged to a programme
that also asked me to run longer repetitions.

### A comparison worth keeping

Two Saturdays used the same format:
**4×300 m / 3 min, then 4×200 m / 90 s**.

On [26 March](https://www.strava.com/activities/6886754696):

- 300s: **51.00 · 49.09 · 48.64 · 47.82**.
- 200s: **29.34 · 30.32 · 28.64 · 30.34**.

On [2 April](https://www.strava.com/activities/6918856301):

- 300s: **47.40 · 47.30 · 47.65 · 46.75**.
- 200s: **29.70 · 30.10 · 30.40 · 29.70**.

The 300s became quicker and more even. The following 200s did not become
quicker on average. That is a more specific observation than saying that the
whole workout improved, and a reason to retain the sequence as well as the
fastest split.

An earlier [12 March session](https://www.strava.com/activities/6811844638)
used **185 m**, with the same two sets and three-minute/90-second rests:
**27.55 · 27.00 · 26.50 · 26.80**, then
**29.05 · 27.60 · 25.95 · 25.75**. The sheet says 200 m for that day.
The completed record keeps 185 m; those times do not become 200 m results.

### Spikes, pacing and the cost of a session

[16 April](https://www.strava.com/activities/6990894269) was my first time
wearing spikes. The session was **1200 m + 600 m + 600 m**, in
**3:38 · 1:42 · 1:50**, or about **3:02/km, 2:50/km, 3:03/km**. I described the shoes as feeling strange and wrote
that my ankles could not hold the pressure on the last repetition. The sheet
marks that Saturday with *SPIKES!*; the activity supplies the actual experience.

<span id="4-may--600-m--400-m--300-m"></span>On [4 May UTC](https://www.strava.com/activities/7086307210),
**600 m + 400 m + 300 m** went **1:37.7 · 59.8 · 42.7**. The 600 and 400 paces were **2:42.8/km and 2:29.5/km**.
I called it brutal training, with “PRs flying all over the place.” Those were
earlier marks in a season that later brought the quicker 600 and 300 time trials
and the June 400 after a 600. The recovery for this May combination is not recorded.

The notes repeatedly record difficulty after a strong opening effort.
On [7 May](https://www.strava.com/activities/7100938650), **1600 m in 5:08**
was followed by **400s in 64 and 71**, with one minute between the 400s. Their paces were **3:12.5/km, 2:40.0/km, 2:57.5/km**.
I described a large fade. On
[23 May](https://www.strava.com/activities/7191949913), **1200 m + 800 m**
went **3:33 · 2:31**, or **2:57.5/km and 3:08.8/km**; I wrote that I burned out on the 800 and vomited at the end.
Those observations belong beside the times. They show why a fast first effort
does not describe the experience of the full session.

### Injuries and the run-up to U20 nationals

My [30 May note](https://www.strava.com/activities/7225286040) calls
**1000 m + 600 m in 2:52 · 1:40**, about **2:52/km and 2:47/km**, the first training after an injury.
On [1 June](https://www.strava.com/activities/7251570407), I was still
recovering, running **300 m / 1 min / 300 m in 42 · 44**. The notes do not
identify a diagnosis.

After the June championships, I began preparing for U20 nationals.
On [17 June](https://www.strava.com/activities/7322435545),
**4×150 m / 6 min** went **19.28 · 19.42 · 19.66 · 19.98**;
I wrote that I was still fighting injuries. The
[18 June](https://www.strava.com/activities/7329423792) mixed-rest 200 session
returned to **4×200 / 3 min + 4×200 / 90 s**. The note summarises the times as
**around 27–28 seconds**, with a severe effort. It does not give eight exact splits.

<span id="22-june--600-m--400-m"></span>On [22 June](https://www.strava.com/activities/7349987474), **600 m + 400 m**
went **1:32.74 · 56.05**, or **2:34.6/km and 2:20.1/km**. I was highly motivated. This is the exact sequence
behind my remembered 56-second 400 after a 1:32 600. The recovery between the
efforts is not supplied.

<span id="27-june--1000-m--600-m"></span>On [27 June](https://www.strava.com/activities/7375436112), **1000 m + 600 m**
went **2:49.29 · 1:34.64**, or **2:49.3/km and 2:37.7/km**. I opened the first 600 of the 1000 at a pace aimed
at 2:40, then faded badly over the final 400. I had slept only three hours and
reported food poisoning. The recovery between those efforts is also absent.

The next substantial effort in the notes is the **1:32.77 600** on 6 July,
after another injury. On
[7 July](https://www.strava.com/activities/7436999152), the final easy session
before racing was **400 m / 1 min / 300 m / 1 min / 200 m / 1 min / 100 m**,
in **75 · 50 · 35 · 16 seconds**. The opening 400 was **3:07.5/km**. I labelled it 1500 m pace. The official
July results then establish what I actually ran at the championships.

### The season did not end on a single day

I have remembered July as the point when regular structured training stopped.
The export preserves later interval sessions too: on
[23 July](https://www.strava.com/activities/7515762396),
**8×400 m / 1 min** went **1:29 · 1:20 · 1:22 · 1:23 · 1:22 · 1:21 · 1:21 · 1:21**. Mean work pace was about **3:26/km**, ranging from **3:20/km to 3:42.5/km**.
On [6 August](https://www.strava.com/activities/7594049315),
**2×2000 m + 2×1200 m + 2×600 m** went
**7:39 · 7:34 · 4:19 · 4:14 · 1:56 · 1:51**, with no recovery supplied. The paired paces were about **3:50/km / 3:47/km** for the 2000s, **3:36/km / 3:32/km** for the 1200s, and **3:13/km / 3:05/km** for the 600s.
On [30 August](https://www.strava.com/activities/7724370524),
**3×600 m / 5 min** went **1:54 · 1:51 · 1:47**, about **3:10/km, 3:05/km, 2:58/km**; I wrote that I was slowly
getting into shape.

Those entries refine the memory of an abrupt July cutoff. They preserve some
later structured sessions without supplying evidence of uninterrupted adherence
to the spring programme. The wider transition belongs with the
[rest of the running record](/running/record/).

## The recovered spring programme

The old sixteen-week sheet has weekdays and week numbers, but no calendar
dates. Six matching dated sessions imply **Week 1 on 7–13 March 2022**:

| Week | Date | Matching record |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 3 | 26 March | 4×300 / 3 min + 4×200 / 90 s |
| 4 | 2 April | The same distances, sets and recoveries |
| 5 | 9 April | 800 m maximum effort → 2:12 time trial |
| 10 | 14 May | 1500 m maximum effort → 4:29.67 time trial |
| 11 | 18 May | 600 m maximum effort → 1:31.49 time trial |
| 11 | 21 May | 1000 m maximum effort → 2:42.79 race |

That is a strong inferred calendar, with recorded differences preserved.
The 12 March repetitions were 185 m against planned 200s. The sheet contains
injury annotations in March, and filled training entries through Week 12.
Its later blank cells cannot describe the June races and workouts, let alone
the July championships: the June 800 even falls on a Sunday marked *REST*.

[The complete dated programme](/running/record/2022/programme/) preserves all
112 weekday entries, the matching evidence and the differences between the
sheet and completed work. In particular, the recovery planned for 600 + 400
on 25 May cannot be transferred to the 22 June session.
