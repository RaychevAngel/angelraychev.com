On 19 September, three 1200 m repetitions exhausted me. On 8 October, the same
workout was fourteen seconds quicker per repetition, with another repetition
definitely available. Repeating the workout lets me see what changed. The notes
keep the bike commute, pacing and felt effort beside the times.

This record includes completed training through **9 October 2026**. The
[notebook](/running-notebook/) explains the ambition and the choices now in force.
The [programme](/running/record/2026/programme/) holds the progression gates and
planned cycle. The sessions below preserve what happened before those plans
are tested.

## Before the September programme

The return did not begin from an entirely empty year. The archive contains an
April **3×1000 m** in **3:56, 3:48, 3:43**, and a **4×500 m** in
**1:35, 1:35, 1:34, 1:33**. The 1000 m times are also their paces per kilometre; the 500s correspond to **3:10/km, 3:10/km, 3:08/km, 3:06/km**. Their recovery was not recorded in the descriptions.
These are earlier interval records, rather than results from the programme
adopted in September. I also remembered self-timed 5Ks around 20:30 earlier in
the year; no exact date for those efforts has been established.

Before the first track session, I also reported about **25 four-minute treadmill
intervals over the preceding fifty days**, at displayed speeds of **10–11.3 mph**.
That is a count of efforts, with no recovered breakdown into sessions or rests.
It gives the track return some immediate training context; the treadmill display
does not establish an equivalent outdoor pace.

## Repeating a protocol

| Protocol | First reference | Later reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3×1200 m / 1 min | [19 Sep](#19-september--lt-31200-m--1-min) · 4:56.33 average (4:06.9/km), exhausted | [8 Oct](#8-october--lt-31200-m--1-min) · 4:42.17 average (3:55.1/km), more work available |
| 3×800 m / 5:30 | [22 Sep](#22-september--vo-3800-m--530) · 2:48 average (3:30/km) | [1 Oct](#1-october--vo-3800-m--530) · 2:44 average (3:25/km) |
| 6×200 m / 3 min | [24 Sep](#24-september--f-6200-m--3-min) · 33.64 average | [5 Oct](#5-october--f-6200-m--3-min) · 32.42 average, last five 31.82 |

The first LT session followed a hard hilly bike ride. The first VO₂ session had
pacing help; my perceived constraint changed on the second. The later F session
used more deliberate effort and movement cues. Each comparison keeps those
conditions with the change in time.

On [9 October](#9-october--vo-3800-m-with-interrupted-timing), the last two full
800s were quicker again: 2:39.4 and 2:39.3. Phone failures left the first finish
estimated and added a partial effort before restarting the second. The dated
note keeps that sequence beside the faster times.

The outer Kezar loops belong outside the 1200 m comparison: distance, surface and
elevation all changed. The 10/10 attempt is an experiment, not a performance score.
The [2021 record](/running/record/2021/#the-repeated-900s) offers an older example
of why pace, rest and the way a session ended need to remain together.

## The completed sessions

### 19 September — LT, 3×1200 m / 1 min

**4:53 · 4:58 · 4:58**

Repetition pace: **4:04.2/km · 4:08.3/km · 4:08.3/km**.

Average: **4:56.33**, or **4:06.94/km**. Total work: 3600 m in 14:49.

I was burned out after the third: stiffening legs and heavy breathing. An
important condition emerged later in the notes: the hard, hilly bike commute
had already left my legs burning before I began. This is the first completed
track LT reference, with that complication attached.

### 22 September — VO₂, 3×800 m / 5:30

**2:50 · 2:49 · 2:45**

Repetition pace: **3:32.5/km · 3:31.3/km · 3:26.3/km**.

Average: **2:48**, or **3:30/km**. Total work: 2400 m in 8:24.

Breathing was hard after about 200 m and felt maximal around 300 m. During
recovery it settled after roughly 2½–3 minutes; the leg burn took longer,
easing around four minutes. Another runner helped pace the third repetition.
I did not add a finishing sprint.

I felt that the legs were constraining the session more than the aerobic
ceiling. That was my experience of the effort, rather than a measured diagnosis.
The 5:30 recovery was part of giving the next repetition a chance to be useful.

### 24 September — F, 6×200 m / 3 min

**33.81 · 33.47 · 33.99 · 33.51 · 33.56 · 33.48**

Average: **33.64**. Spread: **0.52 seconds**.

Relaxed, repeatable fast running. These were not all-out sprints. The consistency
made this a useful initial reference for the F session. It did not tell me how
long I could maintain that velocity continuously.

### 27 September — 2000 m time trial

**7:16.67** — average pace **3:38.34/km**.

| Lap | 400 m split |
| --- | ---: |
| 1 | 1:25.18 |
| 2 | 1:28.64 |
| 3 | 1:29.87 |
| 4 | 1:29.24 |
| 5 | 1:23.74 |

The recorded laps sum to the finish time. I started quickly, backed off, and
did not have a reliable feel for the right pace yet. Breathing was fully heavy
after the second lap; by the fourth I felt spent. I deliberately changed gears
through the final 600, 400, 200 and last 100 metres.

The fast last lap records that finish. It does not by itself establish a large
unused reserve. I thought better pacing might have produced something around
7:13–7:15; the result I actually ran remains 7:16.67.

### 29 September — LT substitute on the outer Kezar loop

The track was closed, so I ran **three repetitions of two asphalt loops**, with
one minute between repetitions. The activity timestamp is **29 September**, while the conversation reported it
just after midnight on the 30th. The activity record resolves the earlier
uncertainty about which day the session belonged to.

| Repetition | First loop | Second loop | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 2:22.27 | 2:31.19 | 4:53.46 |
| 2 | 2:33.93 | 2:32.78 | 5:06.71 |
| 3 | 2:33.21 | 2:33.46 | 5:06.67 |

Total work time: **15:06.84**.

The first loop was too quick; the later laps were steadier. The third repetition
was particularly hard, and a fourth was not available.

My refined Google Earth trace gave **631.89 m per loop**. If my running line
matched that trace, each repetition was about 1264 m and the average work pace
was about 3:59/km. The three estimated repetition paces were **3:52/km, 4:03/km, 4:03/km**. The trace is an estimate of the route, not a measurement of
the exact line I ran. Its 7.42 m difference between minimum and maximum elevation
is a vertical range, not total ascent.

The distance, surface and elevation differ from the track 1200s. I retain this
as a completed session without inserting it into an identical-workout comparison.

### 1 October — VO₂, 3×800 m / 5:30

**2:46.0 · 2:43.5 · 2:42.5**

Repetition pace: **3:27.5/km · 3:24.4/km · 3:23.1/km**.

Average: **2:44**, or **3:25/km**. Total work: 2400 m in 8:12.

The same session on 22 September averaged 2:48. This time it felt more limited
by breathing and less by the muscles. The combination of faster repetitions and
a different felt constraint mattered to me more than the four-second average
alone. It still did not measure how much time I spent near maximal oxygen uptake.

The first graduation gate is a 2:40 average, without a major collapse in
the final repetition. This session remained above it.

### 2–4 October — movement between track sessions

The proposed October 3 speed session moved to October 5. There had been plenty
of activity between the track visits: on October 2 I ran to the gym and back,
then out for food and back. On October 3 I spent much of the day armwrestling
in Vallejo and made a barefoot grocery run that left small abrasions on my feet.
I felt beaten up the next day, when I also ran about a mile with a backpack.
By October 5 my feet felt much better, though my upper body was still sore.

These journeys and the armwrestling explain the delay. They also belong in the
training context even when there is no separate upload for each one. The track
sessions are only part of the load.

### 5 October — F, 6×200 m / 3 min

**35.4 · 31.3 · 31.9 · 31.8 · 32.3 · 31.8**

Average over all six: **32.42**. Average of the last five: **31.82**.

The first was too relaxed. After that I put more deliberate attention into knee
lift, quick leg return and posture, trying to preserve the movement through
each repetition. Maintaining the movement took more effort toward the end of
each repetition, while the later repetitions stayed closely grouped.

This was quicker than the September session, but the effort and mechanical
intent also changed. It was not a same-effort test of improved speed, nor an
all-out 200 m trial.

### 6 October — interrupted 10 s hard / 10 s recovery experiment

Planned: **12 repetitions**. Completed: **5, a 3–5-minute interruption, then 8**.
That is 13 ten-second efforts, or 130 seconds of nominal hard running across
two sets.

I had made an audible timer for the session. The old phone shut down after the
first five repetitions, breaking the intended continuous sequence. After
restarting it, I did eight more.

The effort became brutal early. I could be trying very hard while feeling that
little was happening. Recovery involved several seconds of deceleration, a few
walking steps and then starting again.

The rough displacements of the two sets were about 400 m and 600 m. Those include
movement during recovery; they are not sprint distances. The sets also began
under different conditions, so subtracting one from the other cannot reveal
the distance of the final three efforts.

The session gave me a candidate to investigate, rather than a score to improve.
It helped motivate the programme's **X** slot: an experiment can earn a recurring
place if it proves useful. This particular attempt did not establish the best
format or its eventual training effect.

### 8 October — LT, 3×1200 m / 1 min

**4:42.5 · 4:42.7 · 4:41.3**

Repetition pace: **3:55.4/km · 3:55.6/km · 3:54.4/km**.

Average: **4:42.17**, or about **3:55.14/km**. Total work: 3600 m in 14:06.5.
The fastest and slowest repetitions were 1.4 seconds apart.

This was controlled. I could definitely have done a fourth repetition, and
possibly a fifth. It passed the first LT gate: a 4:48 average with another
repetition plausibly available. The next structure is therefore 3×1600 m;
its eventual 6:08 graduation target does not have to be the pace of my first
attempt at the longer workout.

Compared with 19 September, the average was 14.17 seconds faster per repetition,
with much more reserve. That earlier session followed a hard bike ride, so the
entire difference cannot be assigned to training adaptation.

I was still sore from the F and A combination, but 3:55/km felt relaxed. My
sprinting also seemed to be returning: the stride opening, the knees going
higher. At slower speeds I described needing very little conscious push, and
thought a rhythm around 4:00–4:05/km could feel almost effortless. I thought
the fast work had helped. These observations give me a question to pursue about
movement and economy; I did not measure oxygen cost.

### 9 October — VO₂, 3×800 m with interrupted timing

Planned: **3×800 m / 5:30**. Completed full repetitions:

**~2:43 · 2:39.4 · 2:39.3**

Full-repetition paces: **~3:23.8/km · 3:19.3/km · 3:19.1/km**.

The first finish is a **conservative estimate**, and I think it may have been
faster. I passed 200 m in about 41–42 seconds and 400 m in 1:21–1:22, then tried
to pick up slightly toward the 2:40 target. The phone shut down just as I
finished and was about to press lap. Restarting felt like two to three minutes;
I then timed three more minutes of recovery, giving approximately five to six
minutes in total.

The next attempt reached 200 m in about 40–41 seconds, but the phone died again
at roughly 250–300 m. I was furious. After **60–90 seconds of walking back to
the start**, I restarted the full 800 and ran **2:39.4**. I then rested **5:30**
and ran **2:39.3**. That is three completed 800s, plus the interrupted partial
effort.

The second full repetition was unpleasant; the third was disgusting. I pushed
at the end while keeping my composure, with breathing feeling maximal, and
without a finishing sprint. Another 800 would have required an extreme effort.
The final two nevertheless finished only **0.1 seconds apart**.

I consider this a good session: all three repetitions done, quicker reported
times, and a stable final repetition. I think the extra attempt made it harder.
The phone failures also convinced me to replace phone timing with a watch.

Using the conservative first estimate gives an approximate set average of
**2:40.6**, or about **3:20.7/km**; the missing finish may have been quicker. The 2:40 gate remains
unconfirmed, with **3×800 m / 5:30** retained for the next VO₂ session. Next time,
I want to cement it with reliable timing. The interruptions belong beside the
result without taking away the work completed.

## What the next sessions will test

The completed LT workout unlocks **3×1600 m**, whose later graduation target is
6:08 per repetition. The VO₂ progression remains at **3×800 m**, with a 2:40
average gate. These are independent decisions. Neither demands that the first
attempt at a longer workout already meet its final target.

The 9 October VO₂ session is now recorded above. The proposed 11 October 5000 m
trial, 12 October fast session and first full 18-day cycle remain plans on the
[programme page](/running/record/2026/programme/), awaiting their actual outcomes.
