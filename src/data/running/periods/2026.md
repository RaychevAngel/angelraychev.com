On 19 September, three 1200 m repetitions exhausted me. On 8 October, the same
workout was fourteen seconds quicker per repetition, with another repetition
definitely available. Repeating the workout lets me see what changed. The notes
keep the bike commute, pacing and felt effort beside the times.

On 15 October, the three 800s averaged 2:35.55, comfortably clearing the 2:40
gate. They were faster than the previous attempt and felt more composed.

On 16 October, the first three 1600s averaged 6:23.88, almost exactly 4:00/km.
The early rhythm felt comfortable; direct sun and mounting strain made the
third repetition survival work, before I recovered my posture for a strong finish.

On 19 October, the first three 900s averaged 2:57.00. A smooth opening rep gave
way to heavy legs, a difficult headwind and a finish I could not accelerate.

On 21 October, the first mixed-rest 4+4×200 m averaged about 30.41 per 200 m.
The opening repetitions felt controlled. I held almost the same speed through
the second set, with slipping shoes and increasingly severe muscular strain.

On 23 October, the second three 1600s averaged 6:18.46, or 3:56.54/km.
I ran progressively faster and kept my posture, still in heat and with some
residual soreness from the 200s. Ankle tension returned afterwards and was easing
by the discussion; the next session remains conditional on recovery.

This record includes completed training through **23 October 2026**. The
[notebook](/running-notebook/) explains the ambition and the choices now in force.
The [programme](/running/record/2026/programme/) holds the progression gates and
planned cycle. The sessions below preserve the completed work and the
observations behind the next decisions.

## Before the September programme

The return did not begin from an entirely empty year. The archive contains an
April **3×1000 m** in **3:56, 3:48, 3:43**, and a **4×500 m** in
**1:35, 1:35, 1:34, 1:33**. The 1000 m times are also their paces per kilometre; the 500s correspond to **3:10/km, 3:10/km, 3:08/km, 3:06/km**. Their recovery was not recorded in the descriptions.
These are earlier interval records, rather than results from the programme
adopted in September. I also remembered self-timed 5Ks around 20:30 earlier in
the year; no exact date for those efforts has been established.

Before the first track session, I also reported about **25 four-minute treadmill
intervals over the preceding fifty days**, at displayed speeds of **10–11.3 mph**.
That is a count of efforts, with no recovered breakdown into sessions or rests.
It gives the track return some immediate training context; the treadmill display
does not establish an equivalent outdoor pace.

## Repeating a protocol

| Protocol | First reference | Later reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3×1200 m / 1 min | [19 Sep](#19-september--lt-31200-m--1-min) · 4:56.33 average (4:06.9/km), exhausted | [8 Oct](#8-october--lt-31200-m--1-min) · 4:42.17 average (3:55.1/km), more work available |
| 3×800 m / nominal 5:30 | [22 Sep](#22-september--vo-3800-m--530) · 2:48 average (3:30/km) | [1 Oct](#1-october--vo-3800-m--530) · 2:44 average (3:25/km); [15 Oct](#15-october--vo-3800-m--530) · 2:35.55 average (3:14.4/km), with recorded recoveries of 5:25.67 and 5:20.82 |
| 6×200 m / 3 min | [24 Sep](#24-september--f-6200-m--3-min) · 33.64 average | [5 Oct](#5-october--f-6200-m--3-min) · 32.42 average, last five 31.82; [12 Oct](#12-october--f-6200-m--3-min) · 30.76 average, last two deliberately more sprinty |

The first LT session followed a hard hilly bike ride. The first VO₂ session had
pacing help; my perceived constraint changed on the second. The later F session
used more deliberate effort and movement cues; October 12's final two were
deliberately more sprinty. Each comparison keeps those
conditions with the change in time.

On [9 October](#9-october--vo-3800-m-with-interrupted-timing), the last two full
800s were quicker again: 2:39.4 and 2:39.3. Phone failures left the first finish
estimated and added a partial effort before restarting the second. The dated
note keeps that sequence beside the faster times.

On [15 October](#15-october--vo-3800-m--530), all three full 800s were reliably
timed and below 2:40. Even the slowest, 2:36.27, was 3.03 seconds quicker than
October 9's fastest. Today's mean was 3.80 seconds quicker than the mean of
that session's two recorded finishes, with a more composed ending. October 9's
interruptions and estimated first finish remain part of that comparison.

The [first 3×1600 m](#16-october--lt-31600-m--100-m-walks) on 16 October is a
new reference, rather than another identical-protocol comparison. Each repetition
was a third longer than the 1200s; mean pace was about 4.8 seconds/km slower than
October 8, with longer walking recoveries and substantial reported heat strain.

On [19 October](#19-october--vo-3900-m--nominal-530), the first 3×900 m added
12.5% to the work distance of October 15's 800s. Mean pace was about 2.2 seconds/km
slower, with shorter recorded rests and a strong reported headwind. This is the
opening reference for a new format, with different conditions and execution.

The [21 October mixed-rest 200s](#21-october--fa-4200-m--3-min--4200-m--90-seconds)
add a different fast-running reference. The opening four averaged about 30.31,
against 31.25 for October 12's first four; the second four averaged 30.50 with
shorter rests after a nearly five-minute set break. October 12's 29.30 remains
the faster single repetition. Today supplied more repetitions around thirty
seconds, with a much harder finish and a distance correction kept below.

The [second 3×1600 m on 23 October](#23-october--lt-31600-m--nominal-1-min)
averaged **6:18.46 (3:56.54/km)**, improving on October 16 by **5.43 seconds
per repetition**. All three were quicker, and the third improved by **11.02
seconds**. Recoveries were shorter too. I kept good posture and avoided the
previous breakdown, with less severe heat and some residual muscle soreness.
The progressively quicker finishes were part of the pacing, not metronomic laps.

The outer Kezar loops belong outside the 1200 m comparison: distance, surface and
elevation all changed. The 10/10 attempt is an experiment, not a performance score.
The [2021 record](/running/record/2021/#the-repeated-900s) offers an older example
of why pace, rest and the way a session ended need to remain together.

## The completed sessions

### 19 September — LT, 3×1200 m / 1 min

**4:53 · 4:58 · 4:58**

Repetition pace: **4:04.2/km · 4:08.3/km · 4:08.3/km**.

Average: **4:56.33**, or **4:06.94/km**. Total work: 3600 m in 14:49.

I was burned out after the third: stiffening legs and heavy breathing. An
important condition emerged later in the notes: the hard, hilly bike commute
had already left my legs burning before I began. This is the first completed
track LT reference, with that complication attached.

### 22 September — VO₂, 3×800 m / 5:30

**2:50 · 2:49 · 2:45**

Repetition pace: **3:32.5/km · 3:31.3/km · 3:26.3/km**.

Average: **2:48**, or **3:30/km**. Total work: 2400 m in 8:24.

Breathing was hard after about 200 m and felt maximal around 300 m. During
recovery it settled after roughly 2½–3 minutes; the leg burn took longer,
easing around four minutes. Another runner helped pace the third repetition.
I did not add a finishing sprint.

I felt that the legs were constraining the session more than the aerobic
ceiling. That was my experience of the effort, rather than a measured diagnosis.
The 5:30 recovery was part of giving the next repetition a chance to be useful.

### 24 September — F, 6×200 m / 3 min

**33.81 · 33.47 · 33.99 · 33.51 · 33.56 · 33.48**

Average: **33.64**. Spread: **0.52 seconds**.

Relaxed, repeatable fast running. These were not all-out sprints. The consistency
made this a useful initial reference for the F session. It did not tell me how
long I could maintain that velocity continuously.

### 27 September — 2000 m time trial

**7:16.67** — average pace **3:38.34/km**.

| Lap | 400 m split |
| --- | ---: |
| 1 | 1:25.18 |
| 2 | 1:28.64 |
| 3 | 1:29.87 |
| 4 | 1:29.24 |
| 5 | 1:23.74 |

The recorded laps sum to the finish time. I started quickly, backed off, and
did not have a reliable feel for the right pace yet. Breathing was fully heavy
after the second lap; by the fourth I felt spent. I deliberately changed gears
through the final 600, 400, 200 and last 100 metres.

The fast last lap records that finish. It does not by itself establish a large
unused reserve. I thought better pacing might have produced something around
7:13–7:15; the result I actually ran remains 7:16.67.

### 29 September — LT substitute on the outer Kezar loop

The track was closed, so I ran **three repetitions of two asphalt loops**, with
one minute between repetitions. The activity timestamp is **29 September**, while the conversation reported it
just after midnight on the 30th. The activity record resolves the earlier
uncertainty about which day the session belonged to.

| Repetition | First loop | Second loop | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 2:22.27 | 2:31.19 | 4:53.46 |
| 2 | 2:33.93 | 2:32.78 | 5:06.71 |
| 3 | 2:33.21 | 2:33.46 | 5:06.67 |

Total work time: **15:06.84**.

The first loop was too quick; the later laps were steadier. The third repetition
was particularly hard, and a fourth was not available.

My refined Google Earth trace gave **631.89 m per loop**. If my running line
matched that trace, each repetition was about 1264 m and the average work pace
was about 3:59/km. The three estimated repetition paces were **3:52/km, 4:03/km, 4:03/km**. The trace is an estimate of the route, not a measurement of
the exact line I ran. Its 7.42 m difference between minimum and maximum elevation
is a vertical range, not total ascent.

The distance, surface and elevation differ from the track 1200s. I retain this
as a completed session without inserting it into an identical-workout comparison.

### 1 October — VO₂, 3×800 m / 5:30

**2:46.0 · 2:43.5 · 2:42.5**

Repetition pace: **3:27.5/km · 3:24.4/km · 3:23.1/km**.

Average: **2:44**, or **3:25/km**. Total work: 2400 m in 8:12.

The same session on 22 September averaged 2:48. This time it felt more limited
by breathing and less by the muscles. The combination of faster repetitions and
a different felt constraint mattered to me more than the four-second average
alone. It still did not measure how much time I spent near maximal oxygen uptake.

The first graduation gate is a 2:40 average, without a major collapse in
the final repetition. This session remained above it.

### 2–4 October — movement between track sessions

The proposed October 3 speed session moved to October 5. There had been plenty
of activity between the track visits: on October 2 I ran to the gym and back,
then out for food and back. On October 3 I spent much of the day armwrestling
in Vallejo and made a barefoot grocery run that left small abrasions on my feet.
I felt beaten up the next day, when I also ran about a mile with a backpack.
By October 5 my feet felt much better, though my upper body was still sore.

These journeys and the armwrestling explain the delay. They also belong in the
training context even when there is no separate upload for each one. The track
sessions are only part of the load.

### 5 October — F, 6×200 m / 3 min

**35.4 · 31.3 · 31.9 · 31.8 · 32.3 · 31.8**

Average over all six: **32.42**. Average of the last five: **31.82**.

The first was too relaxed. After that I put more deliberate attention into knee
lift, quick leg return and posture, trying to preserve the movement through
each repetition. Maintaining the movement took more effort toward the end of
each repetition, while the later repetitions stayed closely grouped.

This was quicker than the September session, but the effort and mechanical
intent also changed. It was not a same-effort test of improved speed, nor an
all-out 200 m trial.

### 6 October — interrupted 10 s hard / 10 s recovery experiment

Planned: **12 repetitions**. Completed: **5, a 3–5-minute interruption, then 8**.
That is 13 ten-second efforts, or 130 seconds of nominal hard running across
two sets.

I had made an audible timer for the session. The old phone shut down after the
first five repetitions, breaking the intended continuous sequence. After
restarting it, I did eight more.

The effort became brutal early. I could be trying very hard while feeling that
little was happening. Recovery involved several seconds of deceleration, a few
walking steps and then starting again.

The rough displacements of the two sets were about 400 m and 600 m. Those include
movement during recovery; they are not sprint distances. The sets also began
under different conditions, so subtracting one from the other cannot reveal
the distance of the final three efforts.

The session gave me a candidate to investigate, rather than a score to improve.
It helped motivate the programme's **X** slot: an experiment can earn a recurring
place if it proves useful. This particular attempt did not establish the best
format or its eventual training effect.

### 8 October — LT, 3×1200 m / 1 min

**4:42.5 · 4:42.7 · 4:41.3**

Repetition pace: **3:55.4/km · 3:55.6/km · 3:54.4/km**.

Average: **4:42.17**, or about **3:55.14/km**. Total work: 3600 m in 14:06.5.
The fastest and slowest repetitions were 1.4 seconds apart.

This was controlled. I could definitely have done a fourth repetition, and
possibly a fifth. It passed the first LT gate: a 4:48 average with another
repetition plausibly available. The next structure is therefore 3×1600 m;
its eventual 6:08 graduation target does not have to be the pace of my first
attempt at the longer workout.

Compared with 19 September, the average was 14.17 seconds faster per repetition,
with much more reserve. That earlier session followed a hard bike ride, so the
entire difference cannot be assigned to training adaptation.

I was still sore from the F and A combination, but 3:55/km felt relaxed. My
sprinting also seemed to be returning: the stride opening, the knees going
higher. At slower speeds I described needing very little conscious push, and
thought a rhythm around 4:00–4:05/km could feel almost effortless. I thought
the fast work had helped. These observations give me a question to pursue about
movement and economy; I did not measure oxygen cost.

### 9 October — VO₂, 3×800 m with interrupted timing

Planned: **3×800 m / 5:30**. Completed full repetitions:

**~2:43 · 2:39.4 · 2:39.3**

Full-repetition paces: **~3:23.8/km · 3:19.3/km · 3:19.1/km**.

The first finish is a **conservative estimate**, and I think it may have been
faster. I passed 200 m in about 41–42 seconds and 400 m in 1:21–1:22, then tried
to pick up slightly toward the 2:40 target. The phone shut down just as I
finished and was about to press lap. Restarting felt like two to three minutes;
I then timed three more minutes of recovery, giving approximately five to six
minutes in total.

The next attempt reached 200 m in about 40–41 seconds, but the phone died again
at roughly 250–300 m. I was furious. After **60–90 seconds of walking back to
the start**, I restarted the full 800 and ran **2:39.4**. I then rested **5:30**
and ran **2:39.3**. That is three completed 800s, plus the interrupted partial
effort.

The second full repetition was unpleasant; the third was disgusting. I pushed
at the end while keeping my composure, with breathing feeling maximal, and
without a finishing sprint. Another 800 would have required an extreme effort.
The final two nevertheless finished only **0.1 seconds apart**.

I consider this a good session: all three repetitions done, quicker reported
times, and a stable final repetition. I think the extra attempt made it harder.
The phone failures also convinced me to replace phone timing with a watch.

Using the conservative first estimate gives an approximate set average of
**2:40.6**, or about **3:20.7/km**; the missing finish may have been quicker. At that point, the 2:40 gate remained
unconfirmed, with **3×800 m / 5:30** retained for the next VO₂ session. Next time,
I want to cement it with reliable timing. The interruptions belong beside the
result without taking away the work completed.

### 11 October — 5000 m time trial

**19:59 — 3:59.8/km — 161 World Athletics points**, using the reported finish.

| Kilometre | Split |
| --- | ---: |
| 1 | 3:58 |
| 2 | 4:00 |
| 3 | 4:02 |
| 4 | 4:02 |
| 5 | 3:57* |

The first four kilometres sum to **16:02**, which was also my reported 4000 m
split. Their times span only four seconds. *The final kilometre is the estimate
implied by the accepted 19:59 finish.

This was one of the most unpleasant efforts of my life. My legs felt heavy,
breathing felt completely spent, and after about **2500–3000 m** I could no
longer keep my posture. The knee lift was gone. During the last five to ten
minutes, it felt as if I was crawling through the distance rather than running.
The pace stayed broadly even while the movement became much harder to maintain.

The phone shut down in the **last 50 m**, leaving the finish unrecorded. I had
run a **96-second lap with three laps to go**, then gave everything in the last
two, with an attempted sprint over the final **100–150 m**. I estimated those
last two laps at about **1:35 and 1:33**, with roughly four seconds of
uncertainty. I put the finish at a conservative **19:59**; I thought even a
pessimistic estimate would be around 20:02. The reported result here is 19:59,
with that timing history beside it.

When discussing the 16 October workout, I clarified that I also brought my
posture and knee lift back together over the final two laps of this trial,
especially the last. The miserable middle and the strong finish both belong
in the account.

In the men's outdoor [2025 World Athletics table](https://worldathletics.org/download/download?filename=4f77dcb3-2945-4c58-ad8b-955a999b13e8.pdf&urlslug=World+Athletics+Scoring+Tables+of+Athletics),
19:59 gives **161 points**; 20:00 gives 160. The small difference at twenty
minutes changes little about what this effort tells me.

October 8's controlled LT repetitions averaged **3:55.1/km**, with one-minute

The first longer LT session, on day two of the 18-day cycle that began on

Elapsed time through the third finish was **21:39.57**, over approximately
5 km of work and walking. A further 58.34-second walk followed in the recording.
The work pace almost exactly matches the reported 19:59 continuous 5000 m;
today's elapsed route time includes the recoveries and is not another time trial.

The pace felt comfortable in the early two or three laps. I was thinking
that I would probably speed up in the later repetitions. Instead, I got
cooked under the sun. It felt at least as hot as the previous day's 800s,
but those had two laps of running followed by five minutes in shade. Today
there were four laps, then a short walk still in direct sun. I drank water
in the breaks, without getting relief from the heat.

The last lap of repetition two was already very hard. I felt burned during
the walk and overheated within the opening 200 m of repetition three. Its
second and third laps brought the same loss of posture and knee lift I had
felt during the 5000 m trial. This repetition was survival work.

My approximate checkpoints were **1:37 at 400 m, 3:14 at 800 m and 4:52 at
1200 m**: roughly 97, 97 and 98 seconds per lap. I locked in for the final
lap, went into the red and finished in 6:27. That implies approximately
**95 seconds for the last lap**, using the rounded checkpoints. I recovered
good posture and knee lift as well as speed. This was a harder finish than
I had intended before starting. I felt normal again within 10–15 minutes.

The difficult laps reminded me specifically of laps 7–10 of the 5000 m.
I had also recovered my form for that trial's finish, although it felt much
more miserable. Today made the question of preserving good running through
the difficult middle tangible again. There is a time to push and a time to
back off; learning to stay composed through hard running is part of what I
want to develop. The first 1600 session is complete. **6:08 average remains a
later graduation target**, and no cooler-weather time is inferred.

The inner ankle caused no problems before, during or after the session.
The first rep felt smooth, easy and controlled. Breathing was barely noticeable

strongly. I want to see whether practising that response in the 200s helps me
access it late in longer efforts. That is a training hypothesis to follow.

San Francisco, 5:28 p.m. The track was wet and I could feel my shoes slipping.
The session was intended for October 20, but an event blocked access when I
arrived and I could not return later. I completed it today instead.

The third repetition needs a distance correction. COROS recorded **31.02**.
I ran that repetition in lane three, from the 200 m mark to the opposite-side
200 m mark, covering an extra half-lap arc relative to lane one. Using my reported **42-inch
(1.0668 m) lane width**, the estimated extra distance is
**π × 2 × 1.0668 = 6.702902… m**. At the same average speed, **31.02 over
206.702902… m corresponds to 30.014092… over 200 m**, displayed as **~30.01**.
This is a geometry-based comparison; the original recorded time stays 31.02.
Only this repetition needed the correction; the other seven covered 200 m.

The mean of the eight 200 m comparisons is **~30.41**. The second set was
about **0.18 seconds per repetition slower** than the first, and the full
range was **0.91 seconds**. I opened in 30.47 and finished in 30.46.

Recorded recoveries:

- First set: **2:50.86 · 3:00.52 · 3:00.85**.
- Between sets: **4:56.05**.
- Second set: **1:32.29 · 1:55.52 · 1:25.59**.

The 1:55.52 segment before repetition seven includes an interrupted start.
I began at exactly 90 seconds, accidentally double-clicked the lap button,
noticed the problem about 10 m in, stopped, returned to the start and went
again. The remaining **25.52 seconds** contains that effort and return. It

The first repetitions were nice and controlled. By the fourth, my legs felt
stiff. The second set became brutal. In repetition seven's last 50 m, my legs
felt completely stiff, with heavy, painful squeezing contractions. In the
last repetition, that feeling arrived before I had even left the bend. I
found a way to hold the speed by trying to put more force into the ground
and using more arm action, while feeling my legs give way. I finished
absolutely spent and out of breath; the burning in my legs lasted about

I think this was a good session, and holding the speed was impressive.
The consistent times also conceal how much the effort and movement changed.
Compared with October 12's controlled opening four, today's first set was
about **0.94 seconds quicker per repetition**; the later four stayed close
to that speed with shorter rests. The effort, five-minute set break, wet
surface and interrupted start all belong beside the comparison.

The [19 January 2022 version](/running/record/2022/#19-january--the-mixed-rest-fast-template)

less slowing in the second set. The old set-break duration and effort are
not established, and that winter reference is distinct from June 2022's
roughly 27–28-second version.

Afterwards, I felt tension at the **back of my right ankle**, similar in
location to the earlier left-sided tension. There was **no ankle pain**,
but I changed my walking step as a precaution for a couple of hours. It
felt normal again by the discussion.

I will rest on **October 22**. LT followed by VO₂ is the next intended pair,
subject to recovery. I want another **4+4×200 in X**, with **under thirty
seconds on average for its opening four** as my prediction. Today's first
four totalled about **2:01.254** in 200 m equivalents; the ambition is to go
under 2:00. That is a future target, not a new completed result or formal gate.

This puts the proposed connection to the 900s into sharper focus. I could
keep producing fast running while very fatigued; I also described a severe
late breakdown. Whether repeating this helps me preserve movement and find
a finish in longer efforts remains a question to follow.
