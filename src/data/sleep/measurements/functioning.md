## What counts as a better result?

A higher Sleep Score is a measurable benchmark. Better alertness, sustained attention, learning or training recovery are additional outcomes; they require their own measurements. We should state which improvement an experiment seeks before looking at its result.

The first record has no reported restfulness or daytime sleepiness and no performance assessment. These fields remain unknown. A score of 75 or a Readiness score of 84 cannot supply the missing observations. [First record](/sleep/record/#night-2026-10-13).

## A practical candidate: vigilance

The psychomotor vigilance test (PVT) measures sustained attention through repeated responses to unpredictable stimuli. Its useful outputs include lapses, response speed and false starts, rather than the fastest single reaction.

A published three-minute PVT-B was tested against the standard ten-minute test in 31 adults undergoing total sleep deprivation and 43 undergoing repeated restriction. The shorter version retained useful sensitivity but did not behave identically; its lapse threshold and hardware differed. NASA, NIH and DHS supported the work; an author had a Pulsar Informatics affiliation. A general browser reaction game cannot inherit these results. [Basner and colleagues](https://www.med.upenn.edu/uep/assets/user-content/documents/Basner2011-ValidityandsensitivityofabriefPVT.pdf).

**No personal PVT threshold has been selected.** First choose a credible implementation, characterize its timing behavior and establish repeated performance under documented conditions. Then specify a worthwhile change and an assessment window.

A later calibration analysis simulated latency error in the same sleep-loss datasets. Its proposed timing margins were system bias within ±5ms and latency standard deviation ≤10ms. Those concern the apparatus, not a personal reaction-time target; the analysis does not certify our Mac or a browser implementation. [Timing-accuracy study](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article/44/1/zsaa121/5859160).

## What an assessment must hold steady

Record the implementation, hardware, input method, test duration, time since waking, time of day, caffeine and relevant interruptions. Keep the procedure consistent enough that an apparent gain can be interpreted. Preserve false starts and missing assessments. Faster responses obtained by anticipating the stimulus should not earn a better vigilance result.

A three-minute and ten-minute protocol should remain separate series. If the implementation changes, document the change rather than silently joining the results. A performance test can be useful without establishing clinical impairment or a complete measure of cognitive ability.

## Self-report still answers a separate question

Restfulness, sleepiness, concentration and unwanted fatigue deserve direct reports. They describe the experience we are trying to improve, even when they disagree with a device. Record them prospectively using stable questions and clearly labeled scales; do not infer an unreported feeling from an overnight metric.

Subjective confidence alone should not certify that restricted sleep is sufficient. The [sleep-outcome investigation](/sleep/research/why-sleep-matters/) examines experiments in which measured performance and perceived impairment did not track equally well.

## Connect to training without inventing causation

Running performance, tolerated training and recovery can contribute longer-term outcomes. Individual sessions also reflect training progression, pacing, fuel, motivation and prior workload. A good workout after a high sleep score is an observation, not yet proof that the score caused the workout.

The immediate target is a repeatable outcome series that can distinguish meaningful personal improvements. It gives a later high-score milestone a second question: did the achievement also improve how I function?
