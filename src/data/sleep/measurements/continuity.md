## What each quantity means

Sleep latency is the estimated interval before sleep begins within the selected episode. Wake after sleep onset is waking after initial sleep onset. Total awake time within a bedtime episode can also include initial or final waking. A count of awakenings depends on the event-length threshold.

Sleep efficiency is the ratio of estimated sleep to the selected time-in-bed interval. It does not measure restoration directly. A very short opportunity can yield high efficiency while obtaining insufficient sleep.

## The first comparison

Oura reported 32m10s awake, 7m30s latency and 93% efficiency. COROS reported 13 minutes awake and one awakening longer than five minutes within a slightly shorter episode. We have not reconstructed exact wake-after-sleep-onset values or matched every awakening event.

The Oura efficiency **contributor score** was 98. It is a grade, separate from the **estimated efficiency** of 93%. Its restfulness contributor was 93; that is also a provider grade, not a percentage of an independently measured restorative process. [Contributor definitions](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/360057792293-Sleep-Contributors).

These values give us a measurement question: how much of the difference concerns episode boundaries, quiet waking, short awakenings or algorithm thresholds? The [validation investigation](/sleep/research/measurement-and-validation/) explains why sleep-detection performance can exceed wake-detection performance.

On [14 October](/sleep/record/#night-2026-10-14), Oura estimated 67m38s awake, 10m30s latency and 88% efficiency. COROS estimated 16 minutes awake. The account added cold awakenings and an awakening followed by a precautionary bathroom trip, without strong urinary urgency; their times and durations were not recorded. The bathroom detail was clarified in a dated follow-up, and the initial waking cause remains unknown. Those reports make unwanted disruption worth addressing; they do not validate either device's exact total. Oura's total awake time is not interchangeable with wake after sleep onset.

## The target

Reduce meaningful unwanted disruption or difficulty initiating sleep, while preserving sufficient duration and good functioning. There is no current evidence that the first night requires an intervention to raise an already high efficiency estimate.

Neither zero latency nor zero detected waking is a universal achievement target. A shorter estimate might reflect better initiation, stronger sleep pressure or a different algorithm. A higher ratio can result from cutting the denominator. Both need context before being called an improvement.

For a proposed change, define the primary continuity outcome, event scope and worthwhile difference. Record perceived disruption alongside device estimates; quiet waking can be missed, while movement can be misclassified. Retain duration and daytime effects as checks on the interpretation.

## When the question changes

Persistent difficulties with sleep initiation or maintenance and impaired daytime functioning create a different question from optimizing a healthy sleeper's grade. Clinical assessment and validated treatment pathways may then be relevant. The first two observations do not establish insomnia. [AASM behavioral-treatment guideline](https://jcsm.aasm.org/doi/10.5664/jcsm.8986).
