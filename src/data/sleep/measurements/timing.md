## Three quantities that should not collapse into one

**Clock timing** is when sleep begins and ends. **Regularity** is how stable sleep/wake behavior is across days. **Circadian alignment** concerns the relationship between sleep, internal biological phase, light exposure and behavior. A stable late schedule can be regular; an early schedule can be irregular. A sleep midpoint alone does not directly measure internal phase.

The [first night](/sleep/record/#night-2026-10-13) began around 03:37 and ended at 11:11 PDT. Oura graded timing 12. That shows a weak provider contributor, not that waking at 08:00 is the required personal target. No particular wake time has been adopted.

## What the evidence supports

Regularity is worth measuring independently of duration. A prospective UK Biobank study associated greater Sleep Regularity Index with lower mortality, but its cohort and observational design do not establish a causal benefit from prescribing a young adult an exact clock time. [Windred and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37738616/).

Circadian misalignment has experimental physiological evidence. A small forced-desynchrony laboratory study manipulated the relationship between internal phase and a scheduled day. That is a different exposure from simply choosing a late but stable bedtime. [Scheer and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19255424/).

These findings justify investigating regularity and alignment. They do not supply an independently validated personal minute cutoff or clock-hour optimum. The [strategy investigation](/sleep/research/improvement-strategies/) connects light, caffeine and schedule to specific testable questions.

## What to record first

Retain sleep onset, end and midpoint; differences between days; naps; light exposure when reported; external constraints and daytime functioning. A standard deviation of wake time and the Sleep Regularity Index are not interchangeable: the latter examines sleep/wake state consistency across 24-hour intervals and needs adequate coverage.

A continuous sleep/wake stream is required for a defensible SRI calculation. Daily onset/end summaries provide useful simpler variation measures, but should not be relabeled as a full SRI without defining and justifying the reconstruction.

Oura's timing score can be retained as one provider judgment. Its grading and any chronotype personalization need to be documented separately from observed clock times and directly measured phase.

## How to investigate further

If a phase question becomes consequential, dim-light melatonin onset can assess circadian phase under a controlled collection protocol. It is a different measurement from a questionnaire chronotype or a low timing contributor. Collection conditions, timing and assay validity matter. [Reference-measurement discussion](/sleep/research/measurement-and-validation/#measuring-biological-phase).

The initial target is to characterize the schedule and investigate avoidable misalignment or variation. A later experiment can specify a schedule change and its desired outcome. Choosing a preferred hour before that question is resolved would prematurely define the optimum.
