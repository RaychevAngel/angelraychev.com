## What to measure

Retain estimated time asleep, the selected episode's start and end, time in the episode including awake time, and naps. Sleep opportunity is time deliberately available for sleeping. A device's bedtime boundaries are estimates and need not equal the deliberate lights-out and get-up times in a diary.

On the [first night](/sleep/record/#night-2026-10-13), Oura returned 7h02 asleep within a 7h34m10s episode. COROS returned 7h16 within 7h29. The 14-minute difference in estimated sleep includes both slightly different episode selection and sleep/wake classification. It is not evidence that either estimate is the truth.

On [15 October](/sleep/record/#night-2026-10-15), the diary's approximate 01:40–07:20 interval was 5h40; Oura estimated **5h33m30s asleep** and COROS **5h55** in their selected main episodes. Oura also detected a short evening episode, but I reported no dozing during that interval. It remains a disputed classification, not a confirmed nap. Main sleep, extra episode estimates and recalled opportunity are kept separate.

A midnight-to-07:20 window would offer **7h20**, or 1h40 more opportunity than the reported interval, if that waking time persisted. This is arithmetic for a proposed comparison, not a fixed wake-time target or a guarantee of additional sleep. Midnight itself has not been established as the personal optimum.

On [16 October](/sleep/record/#night-2026-10-16), I reported a sleep attempt roughly at midnight, a remembered awakening around 07:30 to drink water, then more sleep until 09:05. The approximately nine-hour envelope includes an unmeasured awake interval and is not itself a measured total asleep. After synchronization Oura estimated **8h37m30s asleep** within a **9h14m27s episode**, 3h04 more sleep than its preceding main episode; COROS remained pending. The next proposed 22:00–07:00 window would also offer nine hours; it changes timing rather than automatically adding duration.

## The reference and its purpose

Adult AASM/SRS consensus recommends regularly obtaining at least seven hours. NSF guidance gives 7–9 hours for young adults and adults. These are population references, not a demonstrated personal optimum or a rule that more than nine hours is necessarily harmful. [AASM/SRS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4442216/), [NSF](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29073412/).

The direction is sufficient sleep, not indefinitely increasing time in bed. Nor should the objective be the shortest duration that produces a tolerable morning. Repeated restriction experiments found performance impairment that subjective sleepiness did not fully track. The [outcome investigation](/sleep/research/why-sleep-matters/) explains what that experiment establishes and what it does not.

## How a personal target can emerge

Establish repeated obtained sleep and waking performance under ordinary conditions. If shorter nights, sleepiness or inconsistent performance suggest an opportunity, compare a feasible increase in sleep opportunity over repeated blocks while keeping other important exposures reasonably stable.

Choose a primary outcome before inspecting the result. An extra half hour asleep can be a practical improvement even when stage percentages remain unchanged. Conversely, an extra hour in bed without more sleep or better functioning may not earn its burden. Record the difference rather than substituting a success narrative.

The target is currently **regularly sufficient sleep**, with the personal numerical optimum unresolved. The first night's 7h02 is near a reference boundary but cannot establish chronic restriction, sleep debt or adequacy for the present training load.

## Important comparisons

Report main sleep and total daily sleep separately when naps occur. Distinguish sleep opportunity from obtained sleep in experiments. Keep all dates in the assessment window; do not compare a rested holiday week with a demanding work week and attribute the whole difference to bedtime.

Use duration with [continuity](/sleep/measurements/continuity/), [timing](/sleep/measurements/timing/) and [functioning](/sleep/measurements/functioning/). Improving a ratio by reducing the available denominator is not the same achievement as obtaining more restorative sleep.
