## What it represents

Oura's Sleep Score is a composite of total sleep, efficiency, restfulness, REM, deep sleep, latency and timing. Contributors and the composite are graded on a 0–100 scale; 85–100 is the provider's Optimal category. They are not percentages of a physiological process. [Oura score](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/360025445574-Sleep-Score), [contributors](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/360057792293-Sleep-Contributors).

The same night can receive different grades from different devices because both the underlying estimates and the scoring rules differ. Oura gave the [first recorded night](/sleep/record/#night-2026-10-13) 75; COROS gave its selected episode 93. An 18-point difference is not an 18% difference in actual restoration.

## The proposed target

The [target specification](/sleep/targets/#score-milestones) proposes a 14-day average ≥85, then a 28-day average ≥90 with a consistency and coverage requirement. These are explicit project challenges. Their windows and thresholds are choices; no trial establishes that moving from 89 to 90 causes a discrete health improvement.

A score increase can be pursued and celebrated as a score increase. To establish a broader benefit, examine what changed and whether an appropriate outcome changed as well. Examples include obtained sleep, unwanted disruption, a validated vigilance task and reported functioning. A high score with worse daytime performance is a disagreement to investigate, not a reason to erase the latter observation.

Sleep staging validation does not validate every composite-score threshold. A score is downstream of several estimates and an algorithmic judgment. Firmware or scoring changes can alter it without a lifestyle change. Keep hardware and algorithm information when exposed and mark known changes.

Use one named primary score series. Keep COROS as its own series and retain disagreement. Do not select the device with the better grade on each date, average the two scores into a purported biological measure, or merge Sleep Score with Readiness.

The useful record contains the score, all contributors, coverage, context and functional observations. It preserves difficult nights and missingness, so a sustained milestone means what the [assessment rule](/sleep/targets/#score-milestones) says it means.
