## The bottle and the outcome are separate questions

I asked about bottled broth because I prefer drinking it to taking collagen powder. The [main collagen investigation](/nutrition/research/collagen-broth-and-connective-tissue/) explains the earlier experiments and my shoulder context. These additional readings ask what was actually consumed, what changed, and whether that change establishes a useful benefit.

## What one unusually rich broth delivered

A 2019 feeding experiment recruited **15 healthy active men**, comparing six protein sources in randomized order after fasting. Each drink was consumed within five minutes. A deliberately selected rich café broth was assayed after feeding. In **300 mL**, it supplied **11.76 g glycine and 6.42 g proline**, versus **4.90 g and 2.72 g** in the 20 g collagen-peptide powder serving. Those amino acids do **not establish a measured collagen dose in the broth**. [Alcock and colleagues, 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6803522/)

Its blood amino-acid response was more prolonged; protein doses were unequal, limiting direct comparison. The experiment measured neither tendon healing nor symptom relief. Sports-team funding supported it; the authors reported no commercial conflicts. A concentrated café preparation cannot supply the missing composition of my eventual supermarket bottle.

## A positive tendon measurement without an injury outcome

In a professional women's soccer study, **18 athletes were recruited, 15 randomized and 11 completed** ten weeks. They received **30 g liquid collagen** or carbohydrate matched for energy before three weekly training sessions, both with 500 mg vitamin C. Tendon stiffness increased **15.4% versus 4.6%**; the treatment-by-time comparison was significant, with nominal tests across several outcomes. Tendon area, muscle thickness and strength did not show added benefits. The trial did **not demonstrate fewer injuries**. Researchers reported no funding or conflicts; the lead researcher was unblinded. This supports a particular mechanical adaptation, not established shoulder recovery. [Lee and colleagues, online 2024; issue 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12576002/)

The linked [registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06372080) was submitted in 2024 after 2021 collection and describes a broader project. It does not establish prospective priority for this small completer analysis. Liquid collagen remains a different preparation from broth.

## A newer clinical test did not find added benefit

A **January 2026 conference abstract** reports 36 male elite athletes with chronic Achilles, patellar or plantar-fascia tendinopathy randomized to **10 g daily hydrolyzed collagen or maltodextrin**, alongside twelve weeks of heavy slow resistance training. Both groups improved, but primary sport pain and secondary symptoms, function and imaging showed **no significant between-group benefit**; symptom follow-up extended to 38 weeks. [Thomsen and colleagues, 2026](https://bmjopensem.bmj.com/content/12/Suppl_1/A16.3)

This is a relevant clinical null, with a substantial limitation: the inspected report is an abstract. It supplies no complete participant flow, treatment-effect intervals or adverse-event analysis. It does not prove that every collagen preparation is ineffective, or that my reported shoulder has the same condition.

## Improvement within one group can mislead

A separate **2026 male-soccer study** tested **5 g collagen peptides daily for eight weeks** against maltodextrin. Its flowchart shows **16 randomized and 13 analyzed**, although the abstract presents 13 participants. The conclusion emphasizes lower pain and perceived exertion with collagen, but the actual treatment-by-time tests for these scores were **P=.874 and P=.947**. Both groups' perceived exertion fell. Sprint, jump and strength results did not establish added benefit. [Akgün and colleagues, 2026](https://www.jomh.org/articles/10.22514/jomh.2026.020)

A significant change inside the collagen group is not a demonstrated advantage over placebo. The paper also contains inconsistent descriptions of participant flow and pain results. A supplement manufacturer supplied the products. It reports no external funding or conflicts, while the [official registry extract](https://trialsearch.who.int/Trial2.aspx?TrialID=IRCT20240411061470N5) confirms retrospective registration and lists numerous primary outcomes. These limits make the positive headline an unreliable basis for a purchase.

## What changes for my shopping

Broth stays optional. I can log its verified package and amount, leaving unmeasured collagen and glycine unknown. These studies establish neither a personal dose nor a defined shoulder treatment.
