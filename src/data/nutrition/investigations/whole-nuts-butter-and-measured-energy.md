## Almonds and the balance method

Metabolizable energy is estimated from food energy minus energy lost in stool and urine. It is different from burning a food sample in a calorimeter, measuring daily expenditure or observing weight change.

In a controlled crossover study, **18 adults aged 32–67** completed three 18-day periods. Twelve received each of 0, 42 and 84 g/day of whole almonds; six repeated a condition. Foods were scaled using calculated calories, and the final nine days included complete stool and urine collection. The measured average was **129 kcal per 28 g**, compared with **168–170 kcal** calculated using Atwater factors. Individual measurements varied substantially. This was a balance experiment, not a weight-loss trial. USDA and the Almond Board of California supported it. [Novotny et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3396444/).

## A separate walnut experiment

All **18 adults**, averaging 53 years, completed two three-week controlled-diet periods, with or without **42 g/day of walnut halves and pieces**. Researchers reduced other foods proportionately to match calculated calories; this did not establish equal absorbed energy. After adaptation, participants collected stool and urine for about a week.

Measured walnut energy averaged **146 kcal per 28 g**, compared with **185 kcal** calculated from Atwater factors. Metabolizable energy was the registered primary outcome. USDA was the registry sponsor and the California Walnut Board a collaborator. Later microbiome reports used this same cohort, rather than independently repeating the energy experiment. [Baer et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26581681/), [trial registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01832909).

## Whole, chopped and butter

See the [existing whole-versus-butter measurements](/nutrition/research/food-patterns-and-the-food-matrix/#structure-also-changes-how-a-nutrient-total-is-used). **Chopped and whole roasted almonds did not differ significantly**. Pure almond butter contained no added ingredients. USDA and the Almond Board supported the trial. [Gebauer et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27713968/).

## Chewing is another source of variation

A short crossover experiment started with **20 people and analyzed 13**, excluding seven for incomplete attendance, collections or data, or personal withdrawal. Participants ate 55 g of almonds under assigned chewing conditions; all food was provided during subsequent four-day collection periods. More fecal energy was lost with 10 or 25 chews than with 40. However, appetite responses were not a simple progression: the 25-chew condition produced less fullness than both extremes at two hours.

This supports a role for oral processing, not an optimal chewing prescription or a prediction of long-term weight change. The Almond Board funded the study and provided almonds. Registration occurred after the recorded study completion, so it does not establish prospective outcome specification. [Cassady et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19144727/), [registration dates](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00768417).

## What changes in my food log

I enjoyed the whole almonds and walnuts. The recent quantities were rough counts and an approximate equal amount, rather than weighed portions.

These experiments cannot supply my personal absorbed-energy correction. The existing estimated total remains unchanged.

Nut butter remains an optional food choice if I enjoy it and can use the package. Its ingredients, serving amount and label would need their own entry; I should not carry a whole-nut entry over unchanged.
