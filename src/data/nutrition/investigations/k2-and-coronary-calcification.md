I planned to buy vitamin D. I have not reported taking it or K2. The [vitamin K guide](/nutrition/guide/vitamin-k/) keeps that plan separate from my unquantified food menaquinones and explains the earlier bone and valve studies.

## VitaK-CAC: a positive imaging result with important nulls

This **2026 trial randomized 180 patients** with symptomatic coronary disease and calcium scores of 50–400; **167 had baseline data** after postrandomization exclusions. They received **360 µg MK-7 daily or placebo for two years**. Coronary calcium-score growth was lower with MK-7 in the primary analysis. However, the proportions with rapid progression, worsening narrowing and plaque-category changes did **not differ significantly**. Total plaque volume was not measured. The paper’s participant-flow arithmetic is inconsistent. [VitaK-CAC original report](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamacardiology/fullarticle/2850256), [original protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4663571/).

These scans did not establish fewer heart attacks, better survival or more stable plaques. The Dutch Heart Foundation funded the trial; a manufacturer supplied tablets, with no funder role reported. An author disclosed related company grants and shares outside this work. The studied patients differ from a young prevention population.

## DANCODE: the combination improved its primary scan endpoint

The **August 2026 DANCODE report randomized 400 people**, analyzing 398, with a median age of 71 and substantial coronary calcification. It tested **720 µg MK-7 plus 25 µg D3 daily**, versus placebo for two years. Calcium-score progression was **52 Agatston units lower, 95% CI 24–79**. Among 143 with baseline scores below 1,000 and usable angiography, calcified-plaque growth was lower; total plaque-volume progression was similar and noncalcified-plaque differences were nonsignificant. This combination cannot isolate K2’s effect. [Original outcome abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42661461/).

The complete outcome methods and disclosures were inaccessible. The [original protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10351276/) and [current registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05500443) identify overall calcium progression as primary and clinical events as secondary; they establish plans, rather than actual execution. The protocol disclosed free manufacturer tablets, without establishing the final report’s funding. The abstract does not establish event prevention.

## AVADEC: a secondary analysis is not a new randomized trial

A **2023 coronary analysis** selected 304 men from the same AVADEC cohort as the guide’s earlier valve trial. MK-7 plus D did not significantly reduce overall coronary calcium progression: **203 versus 254 units, P=.089**. A higher-calcium subgroup and a small safety-event comparison were nominally positive amid multiple analyses. Only 86 had usable paired plaque scans; no significant plaque-volume advantage was established. These were hypothesis-generating findings, rather than independent randomized replication or convincing event prevention. Foundations and a regional research council funded the study, with free manufacturer tablets and no funder role reported; an author disclosed a vitamin K safety-panel role. [Original secondary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11198368/).

These results justify updating the research account. They do not establish that automatically adding K2 to my planned vitamin D prevents an event in me.
