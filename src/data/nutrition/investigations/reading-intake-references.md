## Different numbers answer different questions

I wanted a clear account of how much of each nutrient is enough, how much is too much, and where the current diet needs attention. A single minimum/maximum table cannot supply that account. The reference system distinguishes planning an adequate diet, estimating the chance of inadequacy, limiting adverse effects and reducing chronic disease risk. The evidence available for each nutrient differs, so the numbers do too.

The [nutrient index](/nutrition/guide/) uses the reference profile I confirmed: **male, 19–30**. Each page keeps the amount estimated from food, the applicable reference and the personal judgment separate. That makes an uncertainty visible instead of forcing it into a reassuring or alarming score.

## RDA: a planning target with a margin for different requirements

The **Recommended Dietary Allowance** aims to cover the requirements of roughly **97–98% of healthy people** in the specified age and sex group. It is a useful target for planning usual intake. A person who eats less than the RDA might still meet their own requirement; being below it on one estimated day does not identify a clinical deficiency. [National Academies explanation of the RDA](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9956/chapter/5).

This margin is part of the design. The RDA is not simply the smallest amount that prevents an obvious disease, and it does not establish that larger intakes improve athletic performance. A therapeutic dose tested for a disease is another question again.

## EAR: the center of estimated requirements, with uncertainty attached

The **Estimated Average Requirement** is intended to meet the requirements of half the healthy people in a group. It helps develop the RDA and assess diets. It is unsuitable as an individual intake goal: at that level, a substantial fraction of the group needs more. [National Academies reference definitions](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9956/chapter/5).

An assessment of an individual's likely adequacy needs an estimate of **usual intake**, variability between days and uncertainty in requirements. Counting how many days fell below an RDA does not replace that assessment. Dietary information can identify a plausible problem worth investigating, while clinical nutritional status also depends on information beyond the food record. [Individual dietary assessment](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9956/chapter/8), [Scope of nutritional assessment](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9956/chapter/3).

## AI: useful guidance when an RDA cannot be established

An **Adequate Intake** is used when evidence is insufficient to estimate an EAR and derive an RDA. It can be based on observed intake in apparently healthy people or other relevant evidence. Usual intake at or above it supports adequacy; intake below it does **not** provide a quantitative estimate of the probability of inadequacy. [National Academies discussion of AI assessment](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9956/chapter/3).

This matters for vitamin K. The reference concerns total vitamin K, while my saved food model quantifies K1 and leaves some sources and other forms unknown. A K1 subtotal below the total-K AI cannot establish a vitamin K deficiency. The [vitamin K page](/nutrition/guide/vitamin-k/) develops the form and measurement issues.

## UL: an upper reference with a specific scope

The **Tolerable Upper Intake Level** concerns usual continuing intake associated with a low risk of adverse effects. It is not an amount to aim for. An intake above it deserves review; the number alone does not establish that injury occurred. When no UL has been established, that usually reflects limited evidence rather than demonstrated safety at every amount. [National Academies UL framework](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9956/chapter/5).

Compare the source scope before comparing amounts. The [magnesium entry](/nutrition/guide/magnesium/) shows this using its total-intake RDA and supplement-only UL.

Similarly, the vitamin A upper reference applies to preformed vitamin A, while the food total is expressed as retinol activity equivalents and includes converted carotenoids. Folate's upper reference concerns folic acid from specified added sources; total food folate in dietary folate equivalents is a different measure. The [vitamin A](/nutrition/guide/vitamin-a/) and [folate](/nutrition/guide/folate/) pages retain those distinctions beside their amounts.

## CDRR and AMDR: risk reduction and dietary planning

The **CDRR** identifies an intake above which reducing usual intake is expected to lower chronic disease risk. Sodium's adult value is **2,300 mg/day**. [Reference tables](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/25353/chapter/28).

The [sodium entry](/nutrition/guide/sodium/) keeps its AI and CDRR beside the food estimate.

The **Acceptable Macronutrient Distribution Range** describes a broad share of dietary energy, such as the adult fat range of **20–35%**. It addresses nutritional adequacy and chronic disease risk in dietary planning. It is not a toxicity threshold or a demonstration of the best performance diet for every athlete. Absolute food amounts, total energy, training and the rest of the pattern still matter. The [fat](/nutrition/guide/fat/) and [carbohydrate](/nutrition/guide/carbohydrate/) pages explain those applications.

## Daily Value: a common reference for labels

The **Daily Value** is a common labeling reference rather than a personalized intake goal. [FDA Daily Values](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-facts-label/daily-value-nutrition-and-supplement-facts-labels).

The [iron entry](/nutrition/guide/iron/) shows why an adult man's RDA differs from the label value.

Labels also use rounding. A declared zero can represent a small amount below the reporting threshold. An assumed zero in a composition database is another kind of evidence. Neither is a laboratory demonstration that the original food contained exactly none. The [food record](/nutrition/record/) and source details distinguish reported labels, current product information and generic estimates. [FDA explanation of label rounding](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/inspection-guides/nutrition-labeling-summary-sheets).

## Different authorities can reach different upper references

The guide's main reference system is the US dietary framework. Where another assessment materially changes the decision, the nutrient page identifies it separately. Vitamin B6 illustrates this: the US adult UL is **100 mg/day**, while EFSA's 2023 assessment established **12 mg/day** for adults, including dietary and supplemental forms. Those are different assessments of neuropathy risk, not interchangeable numbers or recommendations to consume either amount. [NIH B6 upper references](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB6-HealthProfessional/), [EFSA's original assessment](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2023.8006).

The useful response is to retain the authority, date, scope and reason for the difference. Silently replacing a value would make the table look tidier while hiding information relevant to a supplement decision.

## What the attention labels mean

The flags are **decisions about attention**, with a stated reason and review date:

- **No change** means the current evidence supplies no useful reason for a specific adjustment.
- **Review** identifies a plausible issue or worthwhile change to consider.
- **Clarify** means important missing information prevents a useful judgment.
- **Address** is reserved for a credible important problem requiring action.

They are not scores automatically assigned from a percentage. A clearly missing label can deserve clarification even when an estimate looks high. A low single-day food value can deserve review while body status remains unassessed. A genuine clinical finding would require a different record and an appropriately specific judgment.

For my present diet, the useful next step is often ordinary information: confirm a product label or portion, observe repeated days, record an actually taken supplement, or clarify added salt. The structure should help choose that next step without turning every unknown into another item to buy.
