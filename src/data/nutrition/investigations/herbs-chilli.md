## Flavor has already changed the outcome

The reason for choosing chimichurri was the herbs. I also want to look at other herbs and chilli peppers. The earlier overloaded rice, chicken, fish, bean and olive bowl was unpleasant and partly discarded; a later simpler day was enjoyable. Making a manageable meal taste good is therefore a concrete improvement, even before considering a bioactive compound.

The current sauce is a restaurant portion, not a measured herb recipe. A 2 oz container includes some combination of herbs, oil and other ingredients. Its volume does not establish grams of parsley, vitamin K1, polyphenols or oil. The latest calculation keeps the sauce's composition uncertain. It would be misleading to credit a large herb contribution because the sauce looks green, or to treat the entire container as leafy vegetables.

## A culinary dose has been tested in humans

A controlled-feeding crossover trial randomized **71 adults at elevated cardiometabolic risk** to three amounts of a **24-herb-and-spice mixture**, each for four weeks. The doses were **0.5, 3.3 or 6.6 g per 2,100 kcal**. LDL cholesterol, the primary outcome, did not significantly differ between diets. Some secondary 24-hour blood-pressure comparisons favored the highest dose: systolic pressure was **1.9 mm Hg lower than the middle dose**, and diastolic pressure **1.5 mm Hg lower than the lowest dose**. The systolic comparison with the lowest dose narrowly missed significance. Clinic blood pressure, glycemia and vascular-function results were null. [Petersen et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8634565/)

That result concerns a whole mixed-spice feeding pattern, with industry support, rather than one herb or an unmeasured restaurant sauce. Participants' baseline risk also differs from an unmeasured young person's blood pressure. The trial gives culinary use more substance than a list of antioxidant mechanisms, while leaving the individual ingredient and personal benefit unresolved.

## Chilli's metabolic effects should not drive this meal

In a randomized crossover study of **25 healthy, lean adults**, meals containing **1 g cayenne red pepper** increased short-term energy expenditure relative to meals without it. Subsequent food intake was lower in people who did not habitually eat spicy food, but not in regular users. The experiment tested acute responses, not durable weight loss or better training adaptation. Heat preference and adaptation mattered. [Ludy and Mattes, 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21093467/)

This food record already includes meals that were difficult to finish. Choosing chilli specifically to suppress appetite would not address that problem. A pepper or spice that makes chicken, rice or fish more enjoyable may be useful; the amount should follow taste and tolerance. Fresh hot pepper and the cayenne dose in that trial are not interchangeable by equal weight, and neither establishes a treatment for shoulder pain.

## Which herbs are worth trying?

Parsley or cilantro can fit a fresh, bright sauce; basil or oregano can fit tomatoes and chicken; a little chilli can add heat. These are flavor options, not a ranking of medicinal potency. The useful experiment is to find one combination that improves a meal I already eat, instead of buying several herbs to satisfy a biochemical list.

A garnish, a leaf portion and an oil-rich sauce are different food amounts. The current chimichurri has neither a verified recipe nor a quantified herb dose. The [vitamin K entry](/nutrition/guide/vitamin-k/) develops nutrient forms and medication context.

Dried herbs and spices can be a practical option if there is room for a small package and the label's storage instructions work. Fresh herbs are perishable produce and FDA recommends refrigeration. They are a same-day purchase only when they can be kept appropriately and used; the daily-shop arrangement does not make an opened herb pack suitable for indefinite room storage. [FDA: selecting and serving produce](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-produce-safely)

Growing herbs becomes interesting when there is a stable place and routine for them. It is optional future work, not something required to improve tomorrow's meal.

## Keep an extract claim separate

Turmeric, green herbs and chilli often appear in research on concentrated compounds. The food, extracted compound, formulation, dose and tested outcome all need identifying before carrying that result into a menu. NCCIH describes uncertainty around turmeric's clinical benefits and reports liver injury with some highly bioavailable curcumin products. That is a materially different decision from adding a culinary spice for flavor. [NCCIH: turmeric](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/turmeric)

The immediate change is simple: keep an enjoyed sauce or seasoning, record the actual portion and acknowledge the unknown recipe. If the herbs are the main interest, a small known herb portion would make the question clearer. There is no reason here to add large amounts of sauce solely to chase an unmeasured herb exposure.
