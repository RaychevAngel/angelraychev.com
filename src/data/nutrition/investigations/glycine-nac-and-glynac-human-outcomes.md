## A separate question from collagen

I previously took glycine and collagen, and I prefer broth to collagen powder. That history does not establish a current supplement dose or a missing amino acid. The [collagen investigation](/nutrition/research/collagen-broth-and-connective-tissue/) covers the small glycine sleep experiment and broth's uncertain composition.

GlyNAC combines glycine with **N-acetylcysteine (NAC)**, a modified cysteine preparation. Collagen or broth does not reproduce that combination. The question here is whether these particular supplements improved human function, rather than whether their ingredients participate in a useful biochemical pathway.

## Where the older-adult signal began

A 2021 open-label pilot followed **eight adults aged 71–80** during 24 weeks of GlyNAC capsules and 12 weeks after stopping. Eight young adults supplied baseline comparisons and received no supplementation. Walking, strength and several cognitive scores improved during treatment; some changes receded after withdrawal. All participants completed the study.

Without a blinded older-adult placebo group, these observations cannot separate supplementation from time, expectations or repeated testing. The many P values were explicitly unadjusted for multiplicity. This was a reason to run a controlled trial, not proof of rejuvenation or benefit for young lifters. [Kumar and colleagues, 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8002905/)

## What the randomized trial adds—and leaves open

The trial randomized **24 older adults** to GlyNAC or author-described isonitrogenous alanine capsules for 16 weeks; **11 and 12** completed. GlyNAC supplied **100 mg/kg/day each** of glycine and NAC.

Muscle glutathione is the [current registry's primary outcome](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01870193). It increased; gait, grip and chair-rise improved within the GlyNAC group. Functional tables emphasize within-group changes: improvement in one arm and nonsignificance in another do not establish a treatment difference. Six-minute-walk change was **P=.053**; lean mass did not significantly change.

Holm correction covered comparisons within each measurement. Aging-hallmark assays were added after completion; some used three participants per group. A young group received two weeks without placebo. NIH/philanthropic funders were reported uninvolved; authors declared no conflicts. This does not demonstrate lifespan extension. [Original trial, online in 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9879756/)

## The larger trial that missed its main endpoint

Another trial randomized **117 generally healthy older adults**, with **114 completing**, to 14 days of placebo or total daily GlyNAC doses of 2.4, 4.8 or 7.2 g, equally divided between glycine and NAC. Its primary endpoint—whole-blood total glutathione—did **not significantly improve** at any dose versus placebo. Neither did the reduced-to-oxidized glutathione ratio.

A favorable low-glutathione/high-oxidative-stress subset came from **post-hoc analysis**, not a successful primary endpoint. Shorter duration, lower exposure and different measurements limit comparison with Baylor. The null result does not settle other protocols. Nestlé employees authored the study; the disclosure identifies commercial patents/products. Registration followed the reported study period. [Lizzo and colleagues, 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9261343/), [current registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05041179)

## NAC and exercise do not give a simple recovery rule

In a small crossover experiment, **10 men** received NAC or placebo during eight days after **300 eccentric contractions**. NAC reduced some inflammatory markers and the early loss of strength, yet the original abstract reports complete performance recovery only with placebo. Better-looking early markers did not guarantee better later recovery. This was severe experimental muscle damage, not my ordinary low-set lifting session, an injured shoulder or a long-term hypertrophy trial.

The original abstract and indexed methods were accessible; full published methods and supplement files were not. The [registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01778309) was posted after study completion and lists muscle function as secondary. The result warrants caution about the endpoint being optimized, without proving that all NAC use impairs adaptation. [Michailidis and colleagues, 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23719546/)

## Two reports from the same 2026 NAC project

A July biochemical report and September accepted performance manuscript describe **108 randomized and 77 analyzed** participants, with the same registry, dates, flow and baseline characteristics. They represent **one cohort**, not independent replications, using **1.2 g/day NAC** in seven-day crossover periods.

Homocysteine fell; reduced glutathione did not significantly change. The primary **60-minute distance** result was null; selected male perceived-effort, heart-rate and Wingate-average-power findings were positive. Female peak-power genotype interactions yielded no significant pairwise effects.

Wingate preceded the time trial by 20 minutes. Exploratory subgroups, multiple endpoints and uncontrolled training limit interpretation. Registration followed recruitment’s start; historical prespecification remains unestablished. July names biochemical primaries while the [current registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05604586) lists performance. Authors declared no conflicts; only July describes publication-cost funding. [July biochemical report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13414332/), [September accepted manuscript](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-70683-3).
