Urolithin A has genuine randomized human research behind it. The evidence is more interesting than a purely animal-based longevity claim, but less conclusive than the idea of a supplement that renews mitochondria and reliably improves performance. My decision is to keep following it rather than treat it as a required part of the food plan.

## Food precursors and a purified intervention

Urolithin A can be formed by gut microbes after consuming ellagitannins and ellagic acid from foods including pomegranates, certain berries, and walnuts. Conversion varies substantially between people. A randomized crossover bioavailability study in 100 adults compared pomegranate juice with purified urolithin A and found much more consistent exposure from the purified intervention. It measured exposure, not proof of better health. A low value under that study's producer classification is not an established nutrient deficiency. [Original bioavailability study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8821002/).

This creates two separate decisions. I can enjoy walnuts, raspberries, or pomegranate for their food properties without expecting them to reproduce a capsule trial. A manufactured urolithin intervention then needs its own benefit and safety evidence. The fact that microbes can produce a substance from food does not establish that a concentrated supplement is necessary.

## Older adults: positive fatigue testing, null primary outcomes

In a four-month randomized trial, 66 adults aged 65–90 received purified urolithin A or placebo. The final primary outcomes—six-minute walking distance and maximal ATP production in hand muscle—did not improve significantly over placebo. Walking distance increased by 60.8 metres with urolithin A and 42.5 metres with placebo; reporting only the first figure would overstate the result. [Original JAMA Network Open trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8777576/).

There was a more specific positive signal: at two months, participants receiving urolithin A performed more repeated hand and leg contractions before fatigue than placebo participants. At four months, the placebo group had also improved, and the between-group endurance difference was no longer significant. The trial also changed metabolic biomarkers. These are encouraging exploratory findings, but they do not demonstrate better walking ability, durable endurance superiority, or increased lifespan. The paper reports an amendment to the primary outcomes after the study started, with the analysis plan prepared before unblinding.

## Middle age: strength is different from size or aerobic fitness

A separate four-month trial randomized 88 middle-aged adults, predominantly women, with average BMI around 29. The prespecified primary outcome, peak cycling power, was not significantly improved. Selected hamstring strength measures improved compared with placebo; quadriceps measures and grip strength did not show significant between-group gains. Lean and fat mass were unchanged. [Original Cell Reports Medicine trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9133463/).

The aerobic results need particular care. The higher-dose group improved from its own baseline in oxygen uptake and walking distance, but comparisons with placebo did not reach conventional statistical significance: P=0.058 for oxygen uptake and P=0.098 for walking distance. These estimates remain compatible with some benefit, but they do not establish the same result as a positive randomized comparison. The study was explicitly exploratory, with numerous outcomes, and does not show an increase in muscle size.

## Training studies are small and mixed

A 2024 trial in 20 resistance-trained men reported improvements in repetitions to failure and an isometric quadriceps test, alongside some biomarker changes. Bench-press and squat one-repetition maximum did not improve significantly over placebo. The small sample and many tested outcomes make this preliminary rather than a reliable hypertrophy or recovery prescription. [Original resistance-training trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11536656/).

A 2025 trial of 42 competitive male distance runners is closer to an athletic performance question. During four weeks of altitude training, urolithin A did not clearly improve 3,000-metre performance or oxygen uptake compared with placebo. In the time-trial subset of 22 athletes, perceived effort and an indirect muscle-damage marker improved. Those findings could motivate larger recovery trials, but lower creatine kinase is not proof of faster tissue healing or a better race result. [Original runner trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12628386/).

A different 2025 pilot in 20 academy soccer players produced a positive primary result: the urolithin group improved more on an intermittent endurance test, with an estimated 239-metre advantage and a wide 95% confidence interval of 20–454 metres. Jump height also improved, while sprint speed and several power and antioxidant outcomes did not. Participants averaged 17.5 years, the study was single blinded, and only ten players received each intervention. This is a useful positive result alongside the null runner findings, rather than a general conclusion for all athletes. [Original soccer pilot](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1674446/full).

## Immune cells: a new human result with a different endpoint

The 2025 MitoImmune trial randomized 50 healthy middle-aged adults to four weeks of urolithin A or placebo. It found changes in T-cell populations and metabolism, including a 0.50-percentage-point treatment difference in a less exhausted, naive-like CD8-cell population, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.16–0.83. The primary outcomes were cellular measurements. The study did not test whether people had fewer infections, better vaccine protection, less cancer, or longer life. [Original Nature Aging trial](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-025-00996-x).

The investigators also noted that direct activation of classical mitophagy pathways was not formally demonstrated in the treated participants; some supporting laboratory experiments used concentrations above the clinical exposure. The study establishes human biological activity, while leaving the health consequence open. Describing this as proven immune rejuvenation would collapse that distinction.

## Safety, sponsorship, and the decision threshold

The muscle trials generally found acceptable short-term tolerability, without clear safety differences from placebo. Their size and duration cannot settle uncommon adverse events or years of use. Several key trials were funded by the manufacturer Amazentis and included employees, equity holders, or paid advisers as authors. That does not invalidate randomization; it makes independent replication and attention to all prespecified outcomes particularly valuable.

FDA's urolithin A GRAS record concerns specified uses as a food ingredient and records that the agency had no questions about the notifier's conclusion. It is not approval of a drug to slow aging, improve immunity, or treat muscle disease. [FDA GRAS notice 791](https://www.hfpappexternal.fda.gov/scripts/fdcc/index.cfm?id=791&set=GRASNotices).

For my present food-and-training setup, the missing result is a reproducible improvement in a relevant functional outcome with enough follow-up to judge the tradeoff. The evidence does not establish shoulder recovery or a need to replace preferred foods.
