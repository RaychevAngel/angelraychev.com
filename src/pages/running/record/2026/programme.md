This is the programme agreed by **8 October 2026**, with the current decisions
updated through **21 October**. The first cycle began on **15 October**. Its first

**LT** aims at sustainable high lactate turnover. **VO₂** aims at time near
maximal oxygen uptake. **F** is repeatable fast running; **FA** adds a
shorter-recovery fast set; **TT** is a time trial; **X** is an experiment.
These labels describe intentions, not measured internal physiological states.

The larger aim is the notebook's [800+ World Athletics point profile across
800–5000 m](/running-notebook/#the-runner-i-am-trying-to-build). The LT and VO₂
gates below are workout milestones toward that aim. A repetition pace with
recovery is not a continuous race mark to score against the event table.

## The 18-day cycle

Each block has two training days followed by a chill day. Chill leaves room for
rest or easy movement according to how I feel, without another quality session:

| Block | First day | Second day |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | VO₂ | LT |
| 2 | VO₂ | FA |
| 3 | LT | VO₂ |
| 4 | LT | X |
| 5 | VO₂ | LT |
| 6 | TT | F |

That is four LT sessions, four VO₂ sessions, and one each of F, FA, TT and X,
with six chill days.

**The eleven specified workouts stay committed for this cycle. Only X remains
open to a change of format at any point.** LT and VO₂ retain their independently
earned progressions. Changes to the allocation or recurring F/FA formats wait
for the next cycle's review.

**FA** is fast running with a second, shorter-recovery set: the current template
is 4×200 m with three-minute rests, followed by 4×200 m with 90-second rests.
Its purpose includes repeatedly finding fast, organised running with heavy
legs and incomplete recovery: gathering posture and rhythm for the finish.
The first completed session held speed through severe late stiffness; the
near-even times do not establish preserved movement. I see a connection to the
final metres of the 900s; whether that practice transfers remains a question.
**TT** is an event time trial; the transition's 5000 m is complete, and the first
full cycle's proposed endpoint is 800 m. **X** keeps an experimental place open.
After October 21, my intention is to repeat **4+4×200 m** in that slot, subject
to recovery. Dense 10/10 running, 300 m repetitions and 2×3×500 m remain earlier
alternatives. X has not yet been completed. F remains the stable 6×200 m benchmark.

The allocation has some freedom built in. Three LT–VO₂ pairs, F, FA, TT and X
form the backbone; two remaining slots are currently occupied by another LT and
another VO₂. Those could later change if the results justify a different emphasis.
For now, I want to see how this allocation behaves over cycles before changing it
automatically.

The session count does not describe the whole load. A time trial, relaxed fast
repetitions and an experimental sprint set can cost very different amounts. One
recent chill day still included a short backpack run; my plan for another rest day
included running to the gym and back. The label cannot erase that activity.

## Two independently earned progressions

LT and VO₂ advance on separate tracks. A quicker LT session does not require a
simultaneous VO₂ graduation. A calendar does not require either one.

The LT gate is the target average, assessed alongside execution, conditions and
recovery. The October 8 graduation included another repetition plausibly
available; that observation is not a universal requirement to finish every
session with a spare rep. There is a time to push and a time to back off.
The numerical gates remain unchanged. The 1200 m comparison used one-minute
rests; the first 1600 session used the two 100 m walks recorded above.

**A row is a workout and its graduation target.** Passing it unlocks the next
workout; the next row's pace is the next gate, not a compulsory entry pace.
October 8 unlocked **3×1600 m**, and October 15 unlocked **3×900 m**. The first
attempts at those larger sessions do not require their later 6:08 and 2:55.5
graduation times.

The later rows are ambitious proposals, rather than evidence that these exact
steps are optimal or already tolerable. Their role is to make the direction and
next decision clear. The training still has to earn them.

## When to revise it

LT and VO₂ graduate separately when the completed workout meets its time and
intended character. F keeps the same distance, count and recovery while I explore
faster relaxed running. X asks whether an experimental format deserves a recurring
place; an experiment does not need to produce a new personal best to answer that.

The cycle is a way to make the next comparison possible. Actual effort, recovery
and the rest of my movement determine its cost. Running to the gym, carrying a
backpack or a hard bike commute still belongs in that assessment, even on a day
whose programme label says chill.

A revision should keep the previous programme identifiable and point to the
completed observation that changed it. The [spring 2022 sheet](/running/record/2022/#the-recovered-spring-programme)
is an older reminder that a written plan and the season actually run are different
records.
