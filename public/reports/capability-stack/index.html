The Capability Stack

A seven-layer framework for building physical capability that survives decades. It replaces the flat activity×attribute matrix with a stack, because the thing being modeled was never two-dimensional: capacities overlap, labels bundle, order matters, and environment is a stimulus rather than a setting.

§ 00

How to read this

Two conventions carry the whole document, and both exist because of the same worry: that a framework quietly launders one kind of knowledge as another.

1. Color means provenance. Nothing else.

There is no decorative color in this document. Every colored mark identifies which of three evidence streams a claim comes from, and the streams are never merged:

Peer-reviewed. Gives floors, averages, and effect sizes. Trap: it studies what is measurable in 12 weeks.
Anomaly. Outliers and record-holders give existence proofs — ceilings. Trap: survivorship and genetic confounding.
Practitioner. Coaches and physical-culture systems supply protocol detail and hypotheses. Trap: charisma, selection, no controls.

A claim's chip tells you what kind of confidence it earns, before you read the number. Where the streams disagree, the disagreement is reported rather than resolved.

2. Every tool declares its altitude.

The same subject can be described at capacity altitude (“aerobic capacity”), mechanism altitude (“left-ventricular volume, capillary density, mitochondrial density, buffering, lactate shuttling…”), or activity altitude (“run 5k”). None is more correct. Confusing them is how frameworks bloat into unusable spreadsheets, so each tool here states the altitude it operates at: selection runs at capacity altitude, diagnosis at mechanism altitude, scheduling at activity altitude.

§ 01

The stack

Layers compose upward: substrate constrains capacities, capacities express through skills, skills combine into activities, activities are ordered into sessions, sessions accumulate into programs. Environment does not sit on top — it modulates every row simultaneously, which is why it is drawn as an enclosure rather than a layer.

The two distinctions that run through every layer

capacity  vs  skill
A capacity is a general adaptable quality with a physiological substrate; a skill is a learned task-specific pattern. Most classification confusion is these two wearing one label. The tell is transfer: capacities carry across tasks, skills do not.
state  vs  adaptation
State is your condition right now — fatigued, hot, glycogen-depleted, cheered-on, tapered. Adaptation is what chronically changed in you. The same activity in a different state is a different stimulus, which is exactly why sprints after a marathon are not sprints.
Specimen — why the second distinction earns its keep

Maximum heart rate is argued about as if it were binary: textbooks call it a fixed, age-declining ceiling; athletes report it rising with fitness. Both observations are real. The resolution is that HRmax behaves as a fixed ceiling minus a training-state suppression plus an access term. Endurance training lowers measured HRmax by roughly 3–7% (plasma-volume expansion, baroreflex change, sinoatrial adaptation, β-receptor downregulation) and detraining or a taper lifts the suppression back off peer-reviewed; separately, an untrained person's legs fail before the heart's true ceiling is reached, so improved peripheral endurance grants genuine access to beats that were always there.

So a 202 → 205 observation over three years of cycling is most likely a taper-state measurement plus access, not a raised ceiling — and 3 bpm sits inside normal test-to-test variance regardless. Expect other framework “contradictions” to dissolve the same way: as state effects mistaken for adaptations. Source: Zavorsky, Sports Medicine, 2000.

§ 02

The evidence engine

Matching a framework to research is a separate operation from building it, and it is where frameworks most often go wrong — not by ignoring evidence but by treating one stream's authority as if it covered another stream's territory.

Three streams, three jobs, three traps

Peer-reviewed
job: floors, averages, effect sizes

Trap 1 — the streetlight. It measures what fits a 12-week grant with available instruments. Adherence over decades, activity design, and environment interactions are under-studied not because they matter less but because they are hard to fund.

Trap 2 — ergodicity failure. A group mean does not describe an individual trajectory. Force-velocity profiling makes this concrete: two athletes with identical jump height need opposite training, and individualized prescription beat one-size-fits-all in essentially every subject. peer-reviewed

Anomalies
job: existence proofs, ceilings

What only they can tell you: that VO₂max is still trainable at 101, that an 81-year-old can hold 38 ml·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹, that the best performer in history moves ~1.7% when the environment around him is fully engineered.

Trap — survivorship and substrate. The outlier may succeed despite their method, or because of a phenotype you do not have. The 71-year-old marathon record holder ran on 90.3% type I fibers.

Practitioners
job: protocols, hypotheses

Base rate, from the audit: practitioners are repeatedly right about stimuli and variables — progressive overload, depth jumps, loaded carries, end-range loading, effort autoregulation, training frequency — often decades early. They are repeatedly wrong about mechanisms, rigid scheduling, and scope of claims.

Calibration ratio: where practitioner numbers can be checked against controlled trials, they run roughly 1.5–2× the controlled effect. Deflate accordingly rather than discarding.

Triangulation rule

Raise confidence when streams converge from independent directions — a mechanism, a ceiling, and a protocol pointing the same way. Charlie Francis's 48–72 h spacing of high-CNS days practitioner, myofibrillar protein-synthesis timecourses, and elite endurance athletes' spontaneous 80/20 polarization peer-reviewed are three different kinds of argument for the same architecture. That is worth more than three studies of the same design.

The trap specimen every framework should keep on the wall

Olympic medallists outlive matched controls by 2.8 years (relative survival 1.08, n=15,174) — a result quoted everywhere as evidence that athletic training extends life. The same senior author then measured chess grandmasters: relative survival 1.14, statistically indistinguishable from the athletes'. Grandmasters do not train physically.

The most parsimonious reading is that both groups are selected for the same upstream things — cognitive ability, education, socioeconomic status, health behaviors, and the wealth and status that follow elite achievement. This does not undo the dose-response evidence for exercise, which stands on its own. It does mean that “elite athletes live longer” carries almost no causal weight, and that any framework built on outlier populations must ask what else was selected alongside the trait of interest. Clarke, BMJ 2012 · Tran-Duy, PLoS ONE 2018

§ 03  ·  L0

Substrate and ceilings

The substrate layer answers one question: what is the shape of the ceiling, and on which axis? The evidence is emphatic that ceilings are axis-specific. There is no general trainability dial.

L0 design rules

1. Age is not a gate on trainability; treat continuity of stimulus as the state variable and age as a modifier of recovery and injury tolerance only. 2. Economy is a first-class trainable capacity, not a byproduct of fitness — at extreme age it is the main movable variable. 3. Defend complex and explosive capacities explicitly and early; slow strength needs less protection. 4. Genotype informs an expected ceiling on a named axis, never a general score.

The dose ceiling nobody wants in their framework

Above roughly 2000 MET-min/week of lifelong endurance volume, coronary artery calcification prevalence was 68% vs 43% below 1000 (adjusted OR 3.2), and veteran endurance athletes carry ~5-fold odds of atrial fibrillation (OR 5.29). Two honest counterweights: the plaque in the most active men was predominantly calcified — the more stable morphology — and the authors themselves call mortality support for the extreme-exercise hypothesis “primarily circumstantial.” The framework's position is Lazarus & Harridge's set-point: a threshold of activity buys the health; everything above it buys performance. Decide which you are purchasing. Aengevaeren, Circulation 2017

§ 04  ·  L1

Capacities: the atomicity problem

This is the layer your matrix lived on, and the layer where the classification work actually is. Two results bound it. Factor structure is real: Fleishman's program resolved physical proficiency into about nine gross factors, with strength alone splitting into static, dynamic, explosive and trunk components peer-reviewed. But factor structure is also resolution-dependent: the general-motor-ability hypothesis died under Henry's specificity results, then partially returned — a 2018 reappraisal finds a global motor factor that is “emergent and fluid over an individual's lifespan,” strongest in childhood and fragmenting with specialization peer-reviewed.

So the honest answer to “how many capacities are there?” is: it depends who is asking and how trained they are. A framework spanning decades must let its own taxonomy coarsen and refine.

The atomicity test

A label survives as quasi-atomic only if it passes all three. Fail any one and it is a bundle that must be decomposed:

  1. Dissociation. It separates from its neighbours in factor analysis rather than loading on the same factor.
  2. Substrate. It has an identifiable physiological or neural mechanism, not just a behavioural description.
  3. Transfer asymmetry. Training it moves things inside the label more than things outside it.

Fluidity, made quantitative: the transfer radius

You said the systems overflow onto each other, and they do — but the overflow is not uniform, and that is the framework-relevant fact. Transfer breadth is a property of the capacity itself. Force-producing qualities behave like broad general capacities; coordination-dominant qualities behave like narrow bundles of specific skills. So every capacity carries a transfer radius, and the radius shrinks as training age rises — Bondarchuk's central practitioner finding, arrived at by correlating exercise progress with competition results across hundreds of throwers practitioner.

L1 design rules

1. Decompose any label that fails the atomicity test, and place the halves on their correct layers. 2. Attach a transfer radius to every capacity, conditioned on training age. 3. Prefer axes to bins where a continuum exists (force-velocity, duration-intensity). 4. Measure capacities with untrained probe tasks — if you test what you trained, you are measuring a skill.

§ 05  ·  L2

Skills: where the fast things actually live

Everything the atomicity test evicted from L1 lands here, and this layer has a completely different physics: skills are trainable indefinitely, domain-specific, and essentially non-transferable. That combination is good news for a decades-long framework, because skill is the capability that does not decay on the L0 schedule.

  • Anticipation (what boxing coaches call timing) is perceptual-cognitive, learned from thousands of exposures to a specific opponent-action distribution, and confirmed across four decades of temporal-occlusion and eye-tracking work in cricket, tennis, squash and combat sports peer-reviewed. It will not cross sports.
  • Reactive stepping is the fall-arresting skill, and it behaves like a skill: task-specific step training roughly halves fall rates (RaR 0.48), about double the effect of generic balance work. The framework's most important practical consequence for aging is that the thing to train is the recovery step, not the stand.
  • Posture-coupled strength-endurance is real and invisible to gym tests: judo grip-endurance work discriminates competitive level in the dynamic judogi test while simple isometric hang endurance does not practitioner peer-reviewed. “Mat strength” is a legitimate construct that no barbell measures.
  • Environmental skills belong here too, and they can be startling. Glossopharyngeal insufflation — a practitioner-invented lung-packing technique — raised a champion freediver's forced vital capacity from 6.92 L (138% predicted) to 9.04 L (180% predicted) anomaly. Constraints that look anatomically fixed sometimes are not.
L2 design rules

1. Acquire skills fresh — see §07, where fatigue is shown to damage learning, not just execution. 2. Use an external attentional focus: the effect on performance is g = 0.26 but on retention g = 0.58, unmoderated by age, skill level or health status. Cue selection is not coaching garnish; it is the largest single lever in this layer. 3. Expect no transfer and plan accordingly: choose the specific skills you want to own at 80, and practise those.

§ 06  ·  L3

Activities: demand vectors, and inventing the missing ones

An activity is not a capacity and not a skill — it is a demand vector over both, plus a set of environmental couplings. Your original matrix was a demand-vector table; the framework keeps that structure and adds the thing it lacked: a principled way to place an activity relative to a goal, and a procedure for building one when nothing existing fits.

Placement: the four-tier ladder

Bondarchuk's classification is the most developed practitioner answer to this layer and it slots in directly practitioner. Every activity sits at a distance from the target:

TierDefinitionExample, target = keeping yourself upright at 80
GPEGeneral preparatory — different muscles and systemsZone-2 cycling, general hypertrophy work
SPESpecific preparatory — same systems, different movementHeavy split squats, farmer's carries, deep-squat holds
SDESpecialized developmental — same muscles plus part of the target actionTrip-recovery step drills, unplanned-direction change, uneven-terrain running
CECompetitive — the target action and close variantsGetting up off the floor unassisted; carrying something awkward downstairs; a sport with real perturbations

The critical rule attached to the ladder: transfer from the general tiers shrinks as you advance. A novice improves the target by improving anything; a trained person only improves it by moving up the ladder. This is the same phenomenon as the shrinking transfer radius in §04, seen from the activity side.

Designing an activity that does not exist yet

You are right that some activities need to be invented or formally defined. That is a real engineering step, and it has a procedure:

  1. Find the hole. Score your candidate activities as demand vectors and look for capacity/skill combinations that nothing covers. In your matrix the conspicuous holes are reactive-stepping under load, high-velocity work in unpredictable directions, and anything that trains anticipation without head impact.
  2. Write the demand spec before the movement. Name the target capacities, the required velocity or force region, the perceptual demand, the energy-system position, and the environmental couplings.
  3. Apply dynamic correspondence. Verkhoshansky's five criteria are the practitioner transfer test and they work as a design checklist: match the amplitude and direction of movement, the accentuated region of force production, the magnitude of effort, the rate and time of maximal force production, and the regime of muscular work practitioner.
  4. Make it measurable and repeatable. An activity without a score is not an activity, it is a vibe. Define the load, the scoring rule, and the progression before the first session.
  5. Check the debit. Structural risk, joint attrition, and head impact are part of the spec, not an afterthought — especially for anything meant to run for forty years.

Precedent worth knowing: this is exactly what Hébert did in 1912, defining fitness as ten practical movement families (walking, running, jumping, quadrupedal movement, climbing, balancing, lifting, throwing, self-defence, swimming), which MovNat later reorganized into locomotive, manipulative and combative classes practitioner. It is a capability-first taxonomy — the organizing variable is real-world task competence, with capacities developed as by-products. No integrated trial has ever tested such a system as a whole, which is an open problem, not an endorsement.

§ 07  ·  L4

Sessions: order as a variable

Your marathon-then-sprints example is the whole layer in one image, and the evidence supports the intuition — but with a sharper shape than the folklore. Interference is real, small, and confined to specific qualities; it is mostly a scheduling problem, not an incompatibility.

QuestionWhat the evidence saysSource
Does cardio kill gains? Largely no. In 43 studies (n=1,090): maximal strength SMD −0.06 (ns), hypertrophy −0.01 (ns), but explosive strength −0.28 (p=0.007). The explosive decrement appeared in same-session combinations (−0.31) and disappeared when sessions were separated by at least 3 hours. Hickson's alarming 1980 original used 5 d/wk lifting plus 6 d/wk running. peer-reviewed Schumann, Sports Med 2022
Is it the AMPK–mTOR switch? Mostly not. In humans, resistance-exercise mTORC1 signaling is not reliably blunted by prior or subsequent endurance work; the mechanism is multifactorial — residual fatigue, energy availability, total volume. The phenomenon survives; the single-switch story does not. peer-reviewed Coffey & Hawley, J Physiol 2017
Does within-session order matter chronically? Yes, selectively. Resistance-before-endurance produced 6.91% greater lower-body dynamic strength gain over programs of 5+ weeks, with no order effect on hypertrophy, static strength or aerobic capacity. Order matters for high-neural-demand outputs and not for structural or metabolic ones. peer-reviewed Eddens, Sports Med 2018
Can you learn a skill while tired? Badly — and the damage outlasts the session. Practising a novel force-control task after fatiguing exercise impaired acquisition on subsequent fatigue-free days; learners needed roughly twice as long to reach fresh-practice levels. The effect was at least partly central and task-specific. peer-reviewed Branscheidt, eLife 2019
Is heavy pre-loading (PAP/PAPE) worth it? Modestly, for strong athletes, and possibly not at all versus a proper warm-up. Meta-analytic effects are small (jump ES 0.29, sprint 0.51), classical PAP has a ~28 s half-life that cannot explain gains minutes later, and three randomized crossover trials found no jump improvement beyond a general plus muscle-specific warm-up (all SMD ≤ 0.23). peer-reviewed Seitz & Haff 2016 · Rappelt 2024

The two camps are answering different questions

Speed and strength coaching says never practise quality outputs tired. Combat sports and military training deliberately drill under fatigue, because fights and emergencies happen in that state. Both are right, and the peer-reviewed layer adjudicates cleanly: fatigue is a poor environment for acquisition and a legitimate tool for hardening already-consolidated skills, matched to the state of intended use. Better practitioners in both camps already make exactly this split practitioner.

L4 design rules

1. Priority quality goes first, fresh — where “priority” means the most neurally demanding, not the most important-feeling. 2. Separate competing high-intensity stimuli by ≥3 hours within a day and 48–72 hours between high-CNS days. 3. Learn fresh; rehearse tired; never confuse the two. 4. Robust qualities (hypertrophy, aerobic base) are order-insensitive — spend your scheduling budget on the fragile ones.

Convergence worth noting for the 48–72 h rule: it arrives independently from myofibrillar protein-synthesis timecourses, from Charlie Francis's high-low system built by observation in the 1970s–80s practitioner, and from the finding that elite endurance athletes spontaneously settle into ~80/20 polarized distributions with only 2–3 hard sessions weekly. Three streams, three methods, one architecture.

§ 08  ·  L5

Programs: what survives contact with a decade

This layer has the weakest evidence base in the stack and the strongest folklore, so it needs the most discipline.

Periodization: the decisive trial has never been run

Trials compare periodized programs against constant ones, which demonstrates the value of variation — not of pre-planned variation. Comparisons against varied-but-autoregulated programming are essentially absent, so the superiority claims are confounded, and the predicted timing of adaptations has never been directly tested peer-reviewed. Matveyev's phase architecture, which has organized this layer for sixty years, was abstracted from what successful Soviet athletes already did practitioner; its formative assumptions (fixed adaptation timeframes, mandatory capacity sequencing, cross-sport generality) were later judged unsupported.

Framework position: encode sequencing and spacing constraints as hard structure (§07 has real evidence). Treat multi-month scripting as a planning convenience, and prefer autoregulation — RIR-based effort scales have validated construct behaviour (velocity–RPE r = −0.88 in experienced lifters) and are the honest way to run a decade you cannot forecast.

Adherence is the multiplier, not a footnote

63% of new fitness-centre members quit before month three and under 4% remain active past twelve months peer-reviewed. Any program-layer decision that ignores this is optimizing a number that will be multiplied by zero. Two consequences the framework takes seriously:

  • Enjoyment is a physiological variable at this altitude, because it sets lifetime dose. This is the strongest argument for building the program around a sport rather than a protocol.
  • The environment layer's dominant lever is return rate, not the 2% it adds today — see §09.

Honest gap: outdoor and natural-environment exercise reliably improves affect, enjoyment and stated intent to repeat, but the foundational systematic review found no trials measuring long-term adherence at all peer-reviewed. The affect→adherence link is the framework's load-bearing assumption here, and it is an assumption.

§ 09  ·  L6

Environment: stimulus, not setting

You called this a crucial mistake, and the evidence agrees emphatically. Environment enters the framework twice: as a modifier of every stimulus, and as a set of trainable capacities in its own right — heat tolerance, cold tolerance and hypoxic tolerance each have adaptation literatures. It splits into the three sub-layers you named.

Two structural facts this sub-layer forces into the framework

Signed interactions. Environment is not an additive stressor budget. Cold immersion after lifting subtracts from the targeted adaptation, so the framework needs a signed interaction term between exposure and the capacity being trained.

Separate induction and maintenance doses. Environmental adaptations stack wildly different half-lives inside one intervention — plasma volume moves in about a day, haemoglobin mass over five weeks — so unlike most capacity training, induction frequency and maintenance frequency must be specified separately (5/wk to induce, 3/wk to hold).

Best available operational protocol for heat comes from professional cycling, and it specifies things no trial has tested — 7–14 days to induce, 3–4 sessions/week to maintain, total training volume cut 20% during the block, core temp checked every 5 minutes with 39 °C as the working ceiling practitioner. Its convergence with the peer-reviewed finding (clamp temperature, don't count minutes) is real; its effect-size claims run about 1.5–2× the controlled estimates. That ratio is the calibration constant from §02, observed in the wild.

9.2  Sensory-social — four knobs, and an honest deflation

Your stadium-versus-treadmill point is correct, and the mechanism is better than the folklore. But the folklore's founding story needs puncturing first: Triplett's 1898 cyclists — the origin myth of social facilitation — yield only a marginal ~1.8% effect on modern reanalysis, and across 241 later studies the presence of others explains just 0.3–3% of variance, with the sign flipping by task complexity peer-reviewed. Audiences are a small amplifier for what you already do well and a small impairer of what you are still learning.

What actually moves output is more specific:

Information display
the biggest surprise in the layer

Secretly slowing a visible clock by 10% lengthened time-to-exhaustion by ~18% overall and 28–30% in men peer-reviewed. Effort is budgeted against an anticipated endpoint, so endpoint information is a performance variable. A treadmill showing only elapsed time gives you nothing to anticipate against and nowhere for attention to land — which is a plausible mechanism for your observation, though not directly tested.

Competition
unlocks a real reserve

Racing even a crude on-screen avatar improves time-trial performance, and the gain comes almost entirely from increased anaerobic yield with aerobic contribution unchanged — evidence of a centrally regulated reserve. A deceptive avatar set at 102% of a rider's best gets matched; an honest one yields ~1%. Small believable deceptions work; large ones don't; gains vanish once revealed. peer-reviewed

Attentional surface
music, nature, cue language

Music: affective valence g = 0.48, performance g = 0.31, RPE g = 0.22, heart rate unchanged — it works by changing the perceived cost of work, not the physiology, and the benefit attenuates at intensities where interoceptive signals force through. Cue language is the same knob: external focus beats internal, doubly so for retention. peer-reviewed

Stakes — and who is watching
an unrepeatable natural experiment

When COVID emptied stadiums, football home advantage fell ~41% across 4,356 matches — and referee bias toward home teams reversed. Crowds change outcomes partly by moving the evaluators, not only the players. Stakes are constructed by observers, which is why the same workout is not the same stimulus in two rooms. anomaly

Ceiling for the whole layer: Kipchoge ran 1:59:40 in Vienna against his own 2:01:39 world record — a ~1.7% gap produced by total environment engineering (41 rotating pacers in formation, a laser line projecting the target pace, crowds, and zero pacing decisions left to the athlete) anomaly. It is confounded as evidence — drafting is physics, not psychology — but it bounds the prize: even for the best endurance athlete in history, optimizing everything around him is worth under two percent.

L6 design rules

1. Match arousal to task. Audiences and competition amplify well-learned simple expression and impair novel or complex work — so new skills belong in low-evaluation settings with external-focus cues, and maximum expression belongs in engineered high-stakes settings. 2. Amplifiers are acute and they habituate. Reserve competition, crowds and inflated targets for test days. 3. Design endpoint information per session: visible, believable, slightly-ahead targets for hard sessions; hidden clocks and rich sensory fields for easy volume. 4. Judge everyday environments on affect and return rate, not on the 2% they add today. 5. Log environment like load — heat, altitude, terrain, air, fuel state, who was there.

The altitude rule, demonstrated

You cannot maintain 34 activities × 29 mechanisms, and you do not need to. Selection runs at capacity altitude, where a dozen axes suffice. Diagnosis drops to mechanism altitude, but only for the one capacity that is currently limiting. Scheduling runs at activity altitude. The ledger is a diagnostic instrument, not a selection instrument — which is why it should exist for one or two activities, not thirty-four.

They caught real errors in my own researchers' work — including two cases where the cited paper says the opposite of the claim built on it. § 12

Open problems

What the framework does not know, stated plainly, because a framework that hides its holes cannot be improved.

  1. No quantitative model of transfer distance. That strength transfers broadly and balance barely at all is described, not explained. Intermediate qualities — repeat-sprint ability, trunk control, mobility — have poorly mapped radii, and the framework's transfer matrix is currently populated by hand.
  2. Does a general motor factor re-emerge in aging? It appears in childhood and fragments with specialization. If it returns with detraining and age, the taxonomy for a 70-year-old should be coarser than for a 30-year-old — directly consequential here, and untested.
  3. Periodization vs autoregulated variation. The decisive trial has never been run.
  4. Altitude. The foundational live-high-train-low effect has not survived double-blind replication. Whether the gap is hypobaric-vs-normobaric hypoxia, insufficient dose, expectancy, or a real responder subgroup washed out by group means is unknown.
  5. Chronic dose-response for sensory-social amplifiers. Nearly every effect — music, avatars, deception, audience — is an acute crossover finding. Whether repeated exposure habituates the amplifier, builds dependence on it, or trains the reserve itself is essentially unstudied.
  6. Nature and community → multi-year adherence. The affect evidence is solid; the adherence link is assumed. This is the framework's biggest load-bearing assumption at L5.
  7. No integrated practitioner system has been trialed whole. Whether movement-complexity work confers adaptations unavailable from its separated components is unknown — a genuinely open empirical question, not a settled dismissal.
  8. Interaction terms are nearly unstudied. Heat plus hypohydration, cold immersion timing against concurrent goals, whether indoor CO₂ degrades anything beyond mood. The framework predicts these matter and cannot yet quantify them.
What would change the architecture

A validated factor analysis unifying physical proficiency with perceptual-cognitive abilities in one space would collapse the L1/L2 boundary and simplify the stack. A quantitative transfer model would turn L1's hand-populated matrix into something computable. And a long-horizon randomized trial of environment-and-community versus protocol-only programming would settle whether L5's adherence multiplier is as dominant as this framework assumes. Nothing else currently in view would require a redraw.
